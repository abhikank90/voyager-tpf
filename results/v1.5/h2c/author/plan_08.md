## Day by day

**2027-05-15 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Arrival at Istanbul Airport at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Transfer to Historic Peninsula Boutique Stay in Sultanahmet via airport shuttle or metro+tram (approx. 60–90 min). Check in, freshen up, and get oriented.
- Afternoon: Take a gentle orientation stroll around Sultanahmet Square. Admire the exterior of the Blue Mosque and the Hagia Sophia from the plaza — no entry needed yet, just soak in the atmosphere and get your bearings. Visit the nearby Sultanahmet Park to relax after the flight.
- Evening: Wander down to the waterfront along Kennedy Caddesi for sunset views over the Bosphorus and the Sea of Marmara. Return to the neighborhood for a quiet first dinner close to the hotel.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light — jet lag and travel fatigue are real. Buy an Istanbulkart (transit card) at the airport or a nearby kiosk for easy tram/metro access throughout the trip. Exchange some cash or use ATMs for local spending.

**2027-05-16 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit the Hagia Sophia (Ayasofya) when it opens — arrive early (around 8:30–9:00) to beat crowds. Spend 1.5–2 hours exploring this iconic Byzantine-turned-mosque, marveling at the mosaics, massive dome, and layered history spanning 1,500 years.
- Afternoon: Walk to the nearby Topkapi Palace — spend 3–4 hours exploring the imperial treasury, harem quarters, sacred relics, and stunning Bosphorus-view terraces. This was the nerve center of the Ottoman Empire for 400 years.
- Evening: Explore the Gulhane Park adjacent to Topkapi for a relaxing evening walk. Head to the Eminönü waterfront to watch the ferries and grab a famous balık ekmek (grilled fish sandwich) from the floating boats.
- Notes: Topkapi Palace charges separate entry for the Harem — worth it. Wear comfortable shoes; the palace grounds are extensive. Women should carry a scarf for Hagia Sophia.

**2027-05-17 (Monday)**
- Morning: Visit the Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Camii) — free entry, open to tourists outside prayer times. Spend about 45 minutes inside admiring the 20,000 Iznik tiles and six minarets. Then cross the square to the Basilica Cistern (Yerebatan Sarnıcı) — the atmospheric underground Byzantine water reservoir with Medusa head columns.
- Afternoon: Explore the Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı) — one of the world's oldest and largest covered markets with over 4,000 shops. Browse carpets, ceramics, spices, jewelry, and leather goods. Practice your bargaining skills. Spend 2–3 hours wandering its labyrinthine lanes.
- Evening: Walk from the Grand Bazaar down to the Egyptian Spice Bazaar (Mısır Çarşısı) in Eminönü — browse exotic spices, Turkish delight, dried fruits, and teas. Pick up local snacks and souvenirs.
- Notes: Grand Bazaar is closed on Sundays, so Monday is perfect. Bring cash for bazaar shopping — many vendors prefer it. Don't feel pressured to buy; browsing is part of the experience.

**2027-05-18 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Take the tram to Beyoğlu and walk up to Galata Tower — climb to the top for panoramic 360° views of Istanbul, the Golden Horn, and the Bosphorus. Spend about an hour here. Then explore the charming Galata neighborhood with its steep cobblestone streets, independent cafés, and antique shops.
- Afternoon: Walk up Istiklal Avenue (İstiklal Caddesi) — Istanbul's famous pedestrian boulevard stretching 1.4 km through Beyoğlu. Browse bookshops, music stores, clothing boutiques, and pasaj (historic arcades). Visit the Çiçek Pasajı (Flower Passage) and the historic fish market (Balık Pazarı) nearby.
- Evening: Stay in Beyoğlu for the evening — the neighborhood comes alive at night with rooftop bars, live music venues, and meyhanes (traditional Turkish taverns). Enjoy the vibrant local atmosphere.
- Notes: The historic red tram runs along Istiklal Avenue — fun to ride for one stop. Galata Tower can have queues; consider booking online. Beyoğlu is very walkable but hilly — wear good shoes.

**2027-05-19 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit the Istanbul Archaeological Museums (Arkeoloji Müzeleri) — a world-class complex near Topkapi Palace housing the Alexander Sarcophagus, ancient Near Eastern artifacts, and the Museum of the Ancient Orient. Allow 2–3 hours.
- Afternoon: Explore the Süleymaniye Mosque complex — Sinan's masterpiece from the 16th century, set on one of Istanbul's seven hills with sweeping Golden Horn views. The complex includes a külliye (social complex) with a hamam, tombs, and medrese (school). Wander the charming Süleymaniye neighborhood afterward.
- Evening: Head down to the Karaköy district along the Golden Horn waterfront — a trendy, revitalized area with artisan coffee shops, galleries, and seafood restaurants. Walk across the Galata Bridge and watch fishermen casting lines at sunset.
- Notes: The Archaeological Museums are closed on Mondays, so Tuesday or Wednesday onward is ideal. The Süleymaniye area is quieter and more authentically local than Sultanahmet — a nice contrast.

**2027-05-20 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Take a full-day Bosphorus cruise — board a public ferry (Şehir Hatları) from Eminönü for the classic long Bosphorus tour (departs around 10:35, returns ~18:00). The cruise passes under the Bosphorus bridges, past Ottoman palaces, yalı (waterfront mansions), fortresses, and fishing villages on both the European and Asian shores.
- Afternoon: The ferry stops at Anadolu Kavağı (the final stop on the Asian side) for about 2–3 hours. Climb up to the ruined Genoese castle (Yoros Kalesi) for stunning views of where the Bosphorus meets the Black Sea. Browse the small fishing village below.
- Evening: Return by ferry to Eminönü (arrives around 18:00). Enjoy a relaxed evening back in Sultanahmet — perhaps visit a traditional Turkish hamam (bathhouse) for a cultural and relaxing experience.
- Notes: The public Bosphorus ferry (Şehir Hatları long tour) is very affordable (~25–30 TL) and far better value than private tour boats. Buy tickets at the Eminönü ferry terminal. Bring a light jacket — it can be breezy on the water.

**2027-05-21 (Friday)**
- Morning: Cross to the Asian side of Istanbul — take the ferry from Eminönü or Karaköy to Kadıköy. Explore the vibrant Kadıköy market (Kadıköy Çarşısı) — a local food market with fresh produce, cheese, olives, meats, and spices. This is where real Istanbulites shop.
- Afternoon: Wander through the Moda neighborhood of Kadıköy — a charming, bohemian area with tree-lined streets, independent cafés, vintage shops, and a lovely coastal promenade. Enjoy the relaxed, local vibe far from the tourist crowds.
- Evening: Take the ferry back to the European side at sunset — the views of the Istanbul skyline with the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia silhouetted against the sky are spectacular. Return to Sultanahmet for dinner.
- Notes: Kadıköy is one of Istanbul's most authentic neighborhoods — a refreshing contrast to the historic peninsula. The ferry ride itself is a highlight. Friday afternoons can be busy near mosques for prayer — plan accordingly.

**2027-05-22 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Visit the Chora Church (Kariye Camii/Museum) in the Edirnekapı neighborhood — one of Istanbul's greatest Byzantine treasures with extraordinarily well-preserved 14th-century mosaics and frescoes. Then walk along a section of the ancient Theodosian Walls — the 5th-century land walls that protected Constantinople for over 1,000 years.
- Afternoon: Explore the Balat and Fener neighborhoods — the historic Jewish and Greek quarters along the Golden Horn. Wander colorful streets lined with painted wooden houses, visit the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople, and browse antique shops and small galleries.
- Evening: Head back toward Karaköy or Beyoğlu for the evening. Visit the SALT Galata cultural center (free entry) in the former Ottoman Bank building — interesting exhibitions and architecture. Then enjoy the neighborhood's café and bar scene.
- Notes: Balat and Fener are best explored on foot — wear comfortable shoes for the cobblestones. Check Chora Church/Museum opening status in advance as it may have variable access. The area is hilly but very photogenic.

**2027-05-23 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit the Istanbul Modern (İstanbul Modern) art museum in Karaköy — Turkey's premier contemporary art museum with rotating exhibitions by Turkish and international artists. Allow 2 hours. The building has stunning Bosphorus views.
- Afternoon: Take the historic Tünel funicular (one of the world's oldest underground railways, 1875) from Karaköy up to Beyoğlu. Explore the Cihangir neighborhood — a bohemian enclave beloved by artists, writers, and expats, with antique shops, independent bookstores, and cozy cafés. Visit the Firuzağa Mosque area for local neighborhood atmosphere.
- Evening: Return to Sultanahmet for a traditional Turkish hamam experience — try the Çemberlitaş Hamamı (built 1584 by Sinan) or the Çağaloğlu Hamamı (1741). A classic bath, scrub, and foam massage is a quintessential Istanbul experience.
- Notes: Istanbul Modern may have an entry fee (~100–150 TL). Hamam prices vary — expect to pay 300–600 TL for a full treatment at the historic hamams. Book the hamam in advance on weekends. Sunday is a great day for Cihangir as locals are out.

**2027-05-24 (Monday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Princes' Islands (Adalar) — take a ferry from Kabataş or Eminönü to Büyükada, the largest of the nine islands in the Sea of Marmara (about 90 min ferry ride). No cars are allowed on the island — explore by horse-drawn carriage or bicycle through pine forests and Victorian-era mansions.
- Afternoon: Rent a bicycle (very affordable) and ride around the island's perimeter — enjoy sea views, quiet beaches, and the charming village atmosphere. Visit the hilltop Aya Yorgi Monastery for panoramic views of the islands and the distant Istanbul skyline.
- Evening: Return ferry to Istanbul (aim for the 17:00–18:00 departure). Enjoy a relaxed evening in Sultanahmet — perhaps browse the evening market stalls or sit at a rooftop terrace café for tea.
- Notes: Ferries to Büyükada run regularly from Kabataş and Eminönü — use your Istanbulkart. The island gets crowded on summer weekends; weekday visits are more peaceful. Bring sunscreen and a hat — limited shade on the bike route.

**2027-05-25 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Visit the Dolmabahçe Palace in Beşiktaş — the opulent 19th-century Ottoman palace on the European Bosphorus shore, where Atatürk spent his final days. Guided tours cover the crystal staircase, Bohemian chandeliers, and lavishly decorated state rooms. Allow 2–3 hours.
- Afternoon: Walk or take a short bus ride to the Beşiktaş neighborhood — a lively, authentic local district. Explore the Beşiktaş market area, visit the small Çırağan Palace grounds (now a hotel but the gardens and exterior are accessible), and enjoy the Bosphorus waterfront promenade.
- Evening: Take a ferry from Beşiktaş to Üsküdar on the Asian side for the evening — Üsküdar is a traditional, conservative neighborhood with beautiful mosques and Golden Horn views. Visit the iconic Maiden's Tower (Kız Kulesi) lit up at night from the waterfront.
- Notes: Dolmabahçe Palace requires timed entry tickets — book online in advance. Photography is restricted inside. The palace is a 20-minute tram/bus ride from Sultanahmet via Kabataş.

**2027-05-26 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Revisit any missed sights or explore the lesser-visited Little Hagia Sophia (Küçük Ayasofya Camii) — a beautiful 6th-century Byzantine church-turned-mosque near the hotel, often overlooked by tourists. Then visit the Mosaic Museum (Büyük Saray Mozaikleri Müzesi) — displaying stunning Byzantine floor mosaics from the Great Palace of Constantinople.
- Afternoon: Take a cooking class or food tour in the Sultanahmet/Eminönü area — many operators offer 3–4 hour sessions teaching you to make börek, mezes, and Turkish desserts, or guided walking food tours through the bazaar district. This is a perfect way to deepen your food knowledge before leaving.
- Evening: Final major evening out — head to a rooftop restaurant or terrace bar in Sultanahmet or Beyoğlu for dinner with a view. Watch the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia illuminated against the night sky.
- Notes: Cooking classes typically cost 800–1,500 TL per person — book in advance through reputable operators like Istanbul Cooking School or Cooking Alaturka. The Mosaic Museum is small but fascinating and often uncrowded.

**2027-05-27 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Istanbul — take a last leisurely stroll through Sultanahmet. Revisit your favorite spot — perhaps sit in the park between the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia with a final cup of çay. Do any last-minute souvenir shopping at the Egyptian Spice Bazaar or small shops near the hotel.
- Afternoon: Depending on departure time, visit the Topkapi Palace gardens or Gulhane Park for a relaxed final afternoon. Pack up, check out of the hotel, and arrange airport transfer. Allow at least 2.5–3 hours before flight departure for the journey to Istanbul Airport.
- Evening: Departure — fly home with memories of one of the world's most extraordinary cities.
- Notes: Istanbul Airport (IST) is about 45–60 minutes from Sultanahmet by taxi or Havaist airport bus. Allow extra time for traffic. Use remaining TL for airport snacks or spend at the duty-free. Consider buying a jar of Turkish tea, a small tile, or lokum (Turkish delight) as final gifts.
