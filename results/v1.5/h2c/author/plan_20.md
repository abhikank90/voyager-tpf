## Day by day

**2027-04-15 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Arrival at airport at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Clear immigration, collect luggage, and take the Narita Express or Limousine Bus to Tsukiji area (approx. 60–90 min). Check in to your Tsukiji boutique stay and freshen up.
- Afternoon: Take a gentle orientation stroll around Tsukiji Outer Market (仲見世通り周辺). Browse the fresh seafood stalls, kitchen tool shops, and street snack vendors. Pick up tamagoyaki (sweet egg omelette) or fresh oysters from a vendor to snack on and get your bearings in the neighborhood.
- Evening: Dinner at a local izakaya near Tsukiji. Try Tsukiji Itadori or a similar mid-range neighborhood spot for grilled fish, sashimi sets, and cold Sapporo beer. Wind down early to recover from jet lag.
- Notes: Priority on Day 1 is rest and light acclimatization. Avoid heavy sightseeing to counter jet lag. Purchase a Suica or Pasmo IC card at the airport for seamless transit throughout the trip.

**2027-04-16 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Start the day early at Tsukiji Outer Market (opens ~5:00 AM, best visited 7:00–9:00 AM). Join locals and chefs shopping for the freshest tuna, uni, and seasonal produce. Enjoy a classic Tsukiji breakfast: a bowl of fresh tuna don or clam miso soup at one of the market's iconic breakfast counters such as Yonehana or Nakaya.
- Afternoon: Walk or take a short taxi to Hamarikyu Gardens (浜離宮恩賜庭園), a stunning Edo-era tidal garden with a traditional teahouse on the water. Enjoy matcha and wagashi (Japanese sweets) at the Nakajima no Ochaya teahouse. Afterward, stroll along the Sumida River waterfront toward Ginza.
- Evening: Explore Ginza (銀座) for its blend of high-end culture and accessible street life. Visit the Itoya stationery flagship store and browse the Ginza Six atrium. Dinner at Ginza Nair's (legendary Indian-Japanese fusion, affordable for Ginza) or try a tonkatsu set at Ginza Bairin.
- Notes: Hamarikyu Gardens entry fee: ¥300. Tsukiji Inner Market tuna auctions require advance lottery registration — the Outer Market is freely accessible. Wear comfortable walking shoes.

**2027-04-17 (Friday)**
- Morning: Head to Asakusa (浅草) via subway (15 min from Tsukiji). Visit Senso-ji Temple at opening time for a serene atmosphere before crowds arrive. Draw an omikuji (fortune slip), browse Nakamise-dori shopping street for traditional crafts, fans, and ningyo-yaki cakes.
- Afternoon: Walk to the Tokyo Skytree (東京スカイツリー) — a 10-minute stroll from Asakusa. Ascend to the Tembo Deck (350m) for panoramic views of Tokyo. On clear spring days, you may spot cherry blossom remnants and Mount Fuji. Explore the Solamachi shopping complex at the base for Japanese design goods and snacks.
- Evening: Return toward Akihabara (秋葉原) — the heart of Tokyo's tech and otaku culture (20 min by subway). Explore multi-floor electronics stores like Yodobashi Camera, retro game shops, and anime merchandise floors. Dinner at a themed cafe or a reliable ramen shop such as Fuunji-style tsukemen in the area.
- Notes: Skytree Tembo Deck tickets: approx. ¥2,100 adult. Book online in advance to skip queues. Akihabara is best explored in the evening when neon lights are fully lit and shops are busiest.

**2027-04-18 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Shibuya (渋谷). Start at the famous Shibuya Scramble Crossing — experience the organized chaos from street level, then head to Mag's Park or Shibuya Sky observation deck for an aerial view. Visit the Hachiko statue for a cultural moment.
- Afternoon: Explore Harajuku (原宿) and Takeshita Street for youth fashion, crepe stands, and quirky pop culture. Then walk through Omotesando (表参道) — Tokyo's tree-lined 'Champs-Élysées' — for architecture, concept stores, and Japanese designer boutiques. Visit the Omotesando Hills complex designed by Tadao Ando.
- Evening: Head to Daikanyama (代官山) for a quieter, sophisticated evening. Browse the iconic Tsutaya Books (代官山 蔦屋書店) — a beautifully designed bookstore-café hybrid that represents Tokyo's fusion of design and culture. Dinner at a neighborhood bistro or Japanese-French fusion restaurant in Daikanyama.
- Notes: Shibuya Sky observation deck: approx. ¥2,000. Saturday crowds in Harajuku are lively — arrive before noon for a better experience. Tsutaya Books in Daikanyama is open until midnight.

**2027-04-19 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit Meiji Shrine (明治神宮) in Harajuku for a peaceful forest walk through 70,000 trees. Witness a traditional Shinto ceremony if timing aligns (Sunday mornings are common). Explore the inner garden and iris garden (¥500 entry, beautiful in spring).
- Afternoon: Travel to Shinjuku (新宿). Explore Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (新宿御苑) — one of Tokyo's largest parks and a prime cherry blossom / spring greenery spot (¥500 entry). Afterward, explore the Shinjuku government building observation deck (free) for city views.
- Evening: Experience Shinjuku's legendary nightlife district. Walk through Kabukicho (歌舞伎町) and Golden Gai (黄金街) — a maze of tiny atmospheric bars, each seating 5–8 people, many welcoming to tourists. Have drinks at 2–3 Golden Gai bars (cover charge ¥500–¥1,000 per bar). Dinner at a yakitori alley (思い出横丁 Memory Lane).
- Notes: Shinjuku Gyoen is alcohol-free. Golden Gai bars are cash-only — carry ¥5,000–¥10,000 in small bills. Some bars have a 'regulars only' policy but most welcome tourists with a friendly knock.

**2027-04-20 (Monday)**
- Morning: Dedicated tech culture day. Start at the teamLab Planets TOKYO (チームラボ プラネッツ) in Toyosu — an immersive digital art experience blending technology, nature, and culture. Book tickets online in advance (approx. ¥3,200). Open from 9:00 AM.
- Afternoon: Head to Odaiba (お台場) via the Yurikamome monorail — enjoy futuristic waterfront views. Visit the Miraikan (日本科学未来館 — National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation) to explore robots, AI, space tech, and interactive science exhibits. Meet ASIMO-style robots and explore Japan's vision of the future.
- Evening: Stay in Odaiba for sunset over Tokyo Bay and Rainbow Bridge. Explore DiverCity Tokyo Plaza and its iconic life-size Gundam statue (free). Dinner at Decks Tokyo Beach food court or a waterfront restaurant with bay views.
- Notes: teamLab Planets tickets sell out weeks in advance — book online before your trip. Miraikan entry: approx. ¥630. The Yurikamome monorail offers great views of the bay and is covered by Suica card.

**2027-04-21 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Nikko (日光) — a UNESCO World Heritage mountain shrine complex 2 hours from Tokyo by Tobu Nikko Line (approx. ¥1,360 one way). Depart by 8:00 AM. Explore Tosho-gu Shrine, the ornate mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu, surrounded by cedar forests and cascading waterfalls.
- Afternoon: Continue exploring Nikko — walk to Kegon Falls (華厳の滝), one of Japan's most spectacular waterfalls, and stroll along Lake Chuzenji (中禅寺湖) for mountain scenery. Enjoy the peaceful contrast to urban Tokyo.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo by early evening. Relax near Tsukiji — pick up fresh sushi or a bento from the Tsukiji market area for a low-key dinner at the hotel or nearby.
- Notes: Purchase the Tobu Nikko All Area Pass (approx. ¥4,520) for unlimited buses and trains within Nikko. Wear sturdy shoes for temple paths. Tosho-gu entry: approx. ¥1,300. Depart Tokyo no later than 8:00 AM to maximize time in Nikko.

**2027-04-22 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Explore Yanaka (谷中) — one of Tokyo's best-preserved old shitamachi (downtown) neighborhoods that survived WWII bombing. Wander Yanaka Cemetery (beautiful in spring with blooming trees), browse Yanaka Ginza shotengai (shopping street) for handmade crafts, pickles, and traditional snacks.
- Afternoon: Continue to Nezu Shrine (根津神社) — a stunning vermilion torii gate tunnel shrine, less crowded than Fushimi Inari in Kyoto. Nearby, visit the Nezu Museum (根津美術館) in Omotesando for a world-class collection of pre-modern Asian art in a serene garden setting (approx. ¥1,300).
- Evening: Head to Ueno (上野) for the evening. Explore Ameyoko Market (アメ横) — a lively open-air market street with street food, clothing, and fresh produce. Dinner at one of Ueno's many affordable izakayas or Korean BBQ restaurants near the market.
- Notes: Yanaka is best explored on foot — wear comfortable shoes. The neighborhood has excellent independent coffee shops and antique stores. Nezu Shrine's azalea festival (Bunkyo Azalea Festival) often runs in mid-April — check if it coincides with your visit.

**2027-04-23 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit the Tokyo National Museum (東京国立博物館) in Ueno Park — Japan's largest and oldest museum with an extraordinary collection of samurai armor, Buddhist sculpture, ukiyo-e woodblock prints, and ancient ceramics. Entry: approx. ¥1,000. Allow 2–3 hours.
- Afternoon: Travel to Akihabara for a deeper tech culture dive. Visit the Radio Kaikan building (8 floors of electronics, model kits, and collectibles), explore the Super Potato retro gaming store, and browse the latest gadgets at Yodobashi Akiba. Optional: visit a maid café for a uniquely Japanese pop-culture experience.
- Evening: Head to Kanda (神田) and Jimbocho (神保町) — Tokyo's legendary used bookstore district. Browse hundreds of specialty bookshops for vintage manga, Japanese woodblock prints, and rare publications. Dinner at a classic Kanda restaurant — try Kanda Yabu Soba (founded 1880) for premium hand-cut soba noodles.
- Notes: Tokyo National Museum is closed on Mondays. Maid café experiences typically cost ¥1,500–¥3,000 including a drink. Jimbocho bookstores are mostly open until 6:00–7:00 PM.

**2027-04-24 (Friday)**
- Morning: Half-day trip to Kamakura (鎌倉) — 1 hour from Tokyo by JR Yokosuka Line. Visit the Great Buddha (Kotoku-in Daibutsu, ¥300) and Hase-dera Temple with its stunning hillside gardens and sea views. Explore Komachi-dori street for artisan shops and matcha desserts.
- Afternoon: Continue in Kamakura — hike the Daibutsu Hiking Trail through bamboo groves and forest shrines back toward Kita-Kamakura station. Visit Engaku-ji Temple, a serene Zen complex. Return to Tokyo by late afternoon.
- Evening: Spend the evening in Roppongi (六本木) — Tokyo's international arts and nightlife district. Visit the Mori Art Museum (森美術館) on the 53rd floor of Roppongi Hills — the attached Tokyo City View observation deck offers spectacular nighttime panoramas (entry: approx. ¥2,000). Dinner at a mid-range restaurant in Roppongi Hills.
- Notes: Kamakura is a popular day trip — depart Tokyo before 9:00 AM on weekdays to beat crowds. The Daibutsu Hiking Trail takes approx. 1.5 hours and involves some steep sections. Mori Art Museum is open until 10:00 PM on Fridays.

**2027-04-25 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Visit Tsukiji Fish Market area one more time for a proper breakfast at a sit-down sushi counter — try Sushi Dai or Daiwa Sushi (arrive early, expect a queue) for the omakase breakfast set (approx. ¥4,000–¥5,000 — a splurge worth having). Afterward, explore the Nihonbashi (日本橋) historic district nearby.
- Afternoon: Explore the Nihonbashi Mitsukoshi department store — Japan's oldest department store (founded 1673). Visit its basement depachika food hall — a world-class showcase of Japanese culinary culture with artisan wagashi, bento, sushi, and seasonal delicacies. Head to the Coredo Muromachi complex for traditional craft and design shopping.
- Evening: Evening food tour of Shibuya's hidden dining scene — explore Nonbei Yokocho (ノンベイ横丁, 'Drunkard's Alley') in Shibuya for intimate bar-hopping. Then try the Shibuya Parco basement for contemporary Japanese food hall dining. End the evening at a standing sushi bar (tachigui sushi) for a final round.
- Notes: Sushi Dai and Daiwa Sushi queues can be 1–2 hours — arrive by 6:30 AM for a reasonable wait. Alternatively, book a counter seat at a mid-range sushi restaurant via Tableall or Omakase booking platforms. Nonbei Yokocho bars are mostly cash-only.

**2027-04-26 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit Shibuya's cutting-edge tech and culture hub — explore the newly developed Shibuya Stream and Scramble Square complexes. Head to the Shibuya Innovation Base or drop into one of the area's concept stores showcasing Japanese tech design. Visit Loft or Tokyu Hands for innovative Japanese lifestyle products and gadgets.
- Afternoon: Explore Nakameguro (中目黒) — walk along the Meguro River canal, lined with cherry trees (beautiful even post-bloom with fresh green leaves). Browse the independent boutiques, vintage shops, and design studios along the canal. Visit the Starbucks Reserve Roastery Tokyo (スターバックス リザーブ ロースタリー) — a stunning architectural coffee experience.
- Evening: Final major dinner — treat yourself to a memorable meal. Book in advance at a mid-range omakase kaiseki restaurant in Ginza or Nihonbashi (approx. ¥8,000–¥12,000 per person for a multi-course Japanese dinner). This is the ideal night for a cultural fine dining experience.
- Notes: Starbucks Reserve Roastery Tokyo is extremely popular — visit on a Sunday afternoon and expect a wait. Book the kaiseki dinner at least 2–3 weeks in advance via Tableall, Omakase.jp, or Gurunavi. Nakameguro is best explored on foot.

**2027-04-27 (Monday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Tokyo. Return to Tsukiji Outer Market one last time for a farewell breakfast — fresh uni (sea urchin) on rice or a tamago sando (egg sandwich). Pick up last-minute food souvenirs: dashi packets, matcha, wasabi, and premium soy sauce from the market stalls.
- Afternoon: Depending on departure time — visit a nearby convenience store (7-Eleven or Lawson) for final Japanese snack haul (KitKat flavors, Pocky, onigiri). Pack and check out of the hotel. Head to the airport via Narita Express or Limousine Bus (allow 90–120 min from Tsukiji area).
- Evening: Departure. If time permits before leaving, enjoy one final bowl of ramen at Tokyo Station's Ramen Street (東京ラーメンストリート) in the basement — a fitting farewell to Tokyo's food culture.
- Notes: Check out time is typically 11:00 AM — store luggage at the hotel or at a coin locker at Tokyo Station (¥500–¥900/day). Return your Suica card at the airport for a refund of remaining balance (minus ¥220 handling fee). Allow ample time for airport security and immigration.
