## Day by day

**2027-02-15 (Monday)**
- Morning: No activities scheduled — flight LH arrives at 13:00. Travel from the airport to Zermatt via train (Zurich/Geneva → Visp → Zermatt). Zermatt is car-free, so take the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn from Visp for the final leg. Enjoy the scenic mountain views on the way in.
- Afternoon: Arrive in Zermatt (~15:30–16:00 estimated). Check in to the Boutique Stay in Zermatt, Valais. Freshen up, unpack, and take a leisurely stroll along the Bahnhofstrasse to get oriented. Visit the Zermatt village center, browse ski rental shops, and book/confirm equipment rentals for the following days.
- Evening: Explore the charming village on foot. Visit the small but fascinating Matterhorn Museum (Zermatlantis) to learn about the history of Alpine mountaineering — a perfect low-key introduction to the region.
- Notes: Zermatt is accessible only by train (no private cars allowed). Pre-book ski rental from a shop like Intersport or Slalom Sport to save time on Day 2. The altitude (~1,608m) may cause mild fatigue — hydrate well and rest early to acclimatize.

**2027-02-16 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Full day on the slopes — Skiing on Zermatt's Matterhorn Glacier Paradise. Start early with a gondola ride up to Klein Matterhorn (3,883m), the highest ski area in the Alps. Ski the upper glacier runs in crisp morning conditions when snow is best. Enjoy breathtaking views of the Matterhorn up close.
- Afternoon: Continue skiing the Zermatt ski area, focusing on the Rothorn and Sunnegga sectors. These mid-mountain areas offer a variety of blue and red runs ideal for all levels. Take a scenic break at a mountain restaurant around 13:00–14:00 before hitting afternoon runs.
- Evening: Return to the village by gondola (~16:30). Rest and freshen up at the boutique hotel. Take a relaxed walk through the village as the golden-hour light hits the Matterhorn — one of the most photographed moments in Switzerland.
- Notes: Purchase a multi-day Zermatt ski pass in advance online for savings (3–6 day passes offer better value). Wear SPF 50+ sunscreen — UV radiation is intense at glacier altitude. Ski helmets are strongly recommended and available at rental shops.

**2027-02-17 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Second full day skiing in Zermatt — focus on the Gornergrat sector today for a different experience. Take the historic Gornergrat Bahn (cog railway) up to 3,089m for panoramic views of 29 four-thousand-meter peaks. Ski the wide, groomed red and blue runs on this side of the mountain.
- Afternoon: Ski down to Riffelalp or Riffelberg and explore intermediate runs in this quieter sector. For those wanting a break from skiing, the Gornergrat viewpoint offers one of the finest mountain panoramas in Europe — a great spot for photography. Ride the train back down to the village by 16:00.
- Evening: Snowshoe walk around the illuminated village of Zermatt in the early evening — a magical experience as the Matterhorn glows under moonlight. Many marked snowshoe paths are accessible directly from the village.
- Notes: Wednesday is typically less crowded on the Gornergrat sector — a good day to explore this area. The Gornergrat Bahn is a tourist experience in itself and is included with the Zermatt ski pass. Book dinner reservations in advance as popular spots fill quickly in peak season.

**2027-02-18 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Day trip to the Jungfrau Region Ski Safari. Take an early train from Zermatt to Grindelwald (via Visp and Interlaken, ~2.5–3 hours). Begin skiing the famous Jungfrau ski area, starting from Grindelwald-First or the Männlichen plateau. Enjoy the iconic views of the Eiger North Face.
- Afternoon: Continue the Jungfrau Ski Safari, traversing toward Wengen or exploring the connecting runs between Grindelwald and Lauberhorn. The legendary Lauberhorn downhill race course is accessible to confident intermediate skiers. Take the Wengernalp Railway back toward Grindelwald by 15:30.
- Evening: Return journey to Zermatt by train (~2.5 hours). Arrive back at the hotel by 19:30–20:00. Enjoy a quieter evening with a short walk and an early dinner.
- Notes: Purchase a day ski pass for the Jungfrau region separately (~CHF 85–95 for a one-day adult pass). The train journey is scenic and counts as part of the experience — sit on the right side heading toward Interlaken for the best Eiger views. Start early to maximize skiing time given travel.

**2027-02-19 (Friday)**
- Morning: Snowshoeing and Ice Climbing in Engelberg experience — take an early train from Zermatt toward Engelberg (via Visp and Lucerne, ~3 hours). Join a guided snowshoeing trek through pristine powder fields in the Engelberg valley with views of the Titlis glacier. A certified guide leads the group through off-piste terrain.
- Afternoon: Participate in an introductory ice climbing session on a frozen waterfall near Engelberg with the guide — equipment provided. This is a thrilling and accessible experience for beginners and intermediate adventurers alike. Return to Engelberg village by 15:30.
- Evening: Return train to Zermatt (arriving ~19:00–19:30). Relax at the hotel with a hot shower. Spend the evening reflecting on the week's adventures with a quiet dinner.
- Notes: Book the Engelberg snowshoeing and ice climbing guided experience in advance through a local operator like Bergführer Engelberg. Dress in warm, waterproof layers. The train journey through central Switzerland via Lucerne is exceptionally scenic. This is a great active recovery day from intensive skiing.

**2027-02-20 (Saturday)**
- Morning: St. Moritz Bobsled Run Experience — travel from Zermatt to St. Moritz by train (via Chur on the Glacier Express or regional trains, ~3.5–4 hours). Arrive in St. Moritz and head to the legendary Cresta Run, the world's oldest natural ice bobsled track. Experience a thrilling ride down the 1,212-meter track at speeds up to 130 km/h.
- Afternoon: Explore the glamorous village of St. Moritz on foot. Visit the frozen Lake St. Moritz where various winter sports take place. Browse the upscale village — window-shopping along Via Serlas is free and entertaining. If time allows, take a scenic horse-drawn carriage ride on the frozen lake.
- Evening: Return train journey to Zermatt (arriving ~19:30–20:00). Final evening in the Alps — celebrate the trip with a special dinner at one of Zermatt's finest mid-range restaurants.
- Notes: The Cresta Run experience requires advance booking and has age/weight restrictions — check eligibility on the St. Moritz Tobogganing Club website. Saturday trains can be busier — book seats in advance. The Glacier Express route (even partially) is one of the most scenic rail journeys in the world and worth the scenic route if time permits.

**2027-02-21 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Zermatt — rise early for one last sunrise view of the Matterhorn from the village (Matterhorn View Bridge is a perfect free spot). Take a gentle final ski run or a relaxed snowshoe walk on the Sunnegga area for a peaceful send-off. Return ski equipment to the rental shop.
- Afternoon: Check out of the boutique hotel (standard checkout 11:00 — arrange luggage storage if needed). Take the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn from Zermatt back toward Visp and onward to your departure airport. Enjoy the final scenic train ride through the Valais valley.
- Evening: Departure from Switzerland. If the flight is late evening, spend time at the airport or nearby city (Zurich/Geneva) exploring a café or doing last-minute Swiss chocolate and souvenir shopping at the airport duty-free.
- Notes: Allow at least 3.5–4 hours of travel time from Zermatt to Zurich airport (or ~2.5 hours to Geneva airport). Zermatt's car-free policy means all luggage must be manageable on trains. Pick up Swiss chocolate, Läderach pralines, and local cheese at Zermatt shops or the airport as souvenirs. Validate all train tickets before boarding.
