## Day by day

**2027-10-15**
- Morning: Arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport, Kathmandu at 13:00 — clear immigration and collect luggage
- Afternoon: Transfer to Pokhara via tourist bus or shared jeep (approximately 6–7 hours) or take a 25-minute domestic flight to Pokhara if available. Check in to your guesthouse in Pokhara lakeside area and freshen up. Take a short evening stroll along Phewa Lake to acclimatize and orient yourself
- Evening: Explore Lakeside Bazaar, browse trekking gear shops to confirm equipment, and enjoy a relaxed dinner overlooking Phewa Lake
- Notes: Do not schedule anything strenuous today. Focus on rest and hydration. Confirm trekking permits (TIMS card and ACAP permit) at the Pokhara tourism office first thing tomorrow morning. Exchange currency if needed at Lakeside.

**2027-10-16**
- Morning: Early morning visit to the Annapurna Conservation Area Project (ACAP) office and Nepal Tourism Board to obtain your TIMS card and Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP). Then visit Pokhara's World Peace Pagoda for panoramic views of the Annapurna range and Phewa Lake
- Afternoon: Take a short rowboat ride on Phewa Lake to the Tal Barahi Temple on the island. Afterward, visit the International Mountain Museum in Pokhara to understand the Annapurna massif, its ecology, and trekking history — excellent preparation for the days ahead
- Evening: Pack your trekking bag carefully, laying out gear for the trek. Enjoy a leisurely dinner and an early night to prepare for the trek start tomorrow
- Notes: Permits are mandatory and must be obtained before entering the conservation area. Costs: TIMS ~USD 20, ACAP ~USD 30. Confirm your trek route with your guide or guesthouse — the classic Annapurna Sanctuary (Base Camp) route is recommended for 14 days.

**2027-10-17**
- Morning: Depart Pokhara by local bus or jeep to Nayapul (approximately 1.5–2 hours). Begin trekking from Nayapul through Birethanti, crossing the Modi Khola suspension bridge and following the river upstream through terraced fields and rhododendron forests toward Tikhedhunga
- Afternoon: Continue trekking through Ulleri, climbing the famous stone staircase of approximately 3,000 steps — a challenging but rewarding ascent through traditional Magar villages. Arrive at Ghorepani or stop at Ulleri for the night depending on pace
- Evening: Arrive at Ulleri or Banthanti teahouse, settle in, and enjoy the cool mountain air. Socialize with fellow trekkers over hot tea
- Notes: Today's trek: Nayapul to Ulleri, approximately 9 km, 4–5 hours, elevation gain ~1,960m to ~2,070m. Pace yourself on the stone staircase. Drink plenty of water. October is peak season — teahouses are busy so arrive by 3–4 PM to secure a room.

**2027-10-18**
- Morning: Wake before dawn and hike from Ulleri (or Banthanti) through dense rhododendron and oak forests to Ghorepani (2,860m). The trail is moderate with beautiful forest scenery. Arrive at Ghorepani by mid-morning and drop your pack at the teahouse
- Afternoon: Hike up to Poon Hill (3,210m) — about 45 minutes from Ghorepani. Enjoy sweeping panoramic views of Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Machhapuchhre (Fishtail), and the surrounding peaks. Note: Many trekkers do this at sunrise the next day, so afternoon visit avoids crowds
- Evening: Return to Ghorepani for the evening. Warm up by the teahouse fire, meet fellow trekkers, and enjoy the mountain atmosphere
- Notes: Ulleri to Ghorepani: approximately 8 km, 3–4 hours. Altitude increases to 2,860m — watch for early signs of altitude sickness (headache, nausea). Drink 3–4 liters of water today. Set an alarm for tomorrow's sunrise hike to Poon Hill (4:30 AM departure recommended).

**2027-10-19**
- Morning: Rise at 4:30 AM and hike to Poon Hill (3,210m) by headlamp for a spectacular sunrise over the Annapurna and Dhaulagiri ranges — one of the most iconic views in Nepal. Return to Ghorepani for breakfast, then continue trekking to Tadapani through magnificent rhododendron forests
- Afternoon: Arrive at Tadapani (2,630m) by early afternoon. Enjoy views of Annapurna South and Hiunchuli from the village. Rest and explore the small settlement. Optional: short walk to a nearby viewpoint above Tadapani
- Evening: Settle into your teahouse, enjoy the quieter atmosphere of Tadapani compared to Ghorepani, and rest well for tomorrow's longer stage
- Notes: Ghorepani to Tadapani: approximately 10 km, 4–5 hours. The rhododendron forest section between Deurali and Tadapani is stunning even in October. Trail can be muddy — trekking poles recommended. Tadapani has fewer teahouses so arrive early.

**2027-10-20**
- Morning: Trek from Tadapani down through terraced farmland and forest to Chhomrong (2,170m), the last major permanent village before the Annapurna Sanctuary. The descent offers beautiful views of Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre. Cross the Chhomrong Khola river and climb the stone steps up into the village
- Afternoon: Arrive at Chhomrong and explore this charming Gurung village. Visit the ACAP checkpoint to have permits stamped. Take a short walk to the upper village for views of Annapurna South towering directly above. Rest and recover from the day's trek
- Evening: Enjoy the vibrant teahouse scene in Chhomrong — it is a hub for trekkers heading in and out of the Sanctuary. Hot shower available at most guesthouses here (last reliable hot shower for several days)
- Notes: Tadapani to Chhomrong: approximately 10 km, 4–5 hours. The stone staircase into Chhomrong is steep and tiring at the end of a long day — take it slowly. Chhomrong is the last place to stock up on snacks, chocolate, and supplies before the upper Sanctuary. Prices increase significantly above here.

**2027-10-21**
- Morning: Trek from Chhomrong descending to Chhomrong Khola then ascending steeply through bamboo and rhododendron forest to Sinuwa (2,360m) and onward to Bamboo (2,310m). The trail enters the increasingly dramatic Modi Khola gorge
- Afternoon: Continue from Bamboo through dense bamboo groves and forest to Dovan (2,600m). The gorge narrows and the trail becomes more rugged. Arrive at Dovan teahouses and settle in — the accommodation is more basic here but comfortable enough
- Evening: Enjoy the dramatic gorge setting with the roar of the Modi Khola river. The forest is alive with bird calls. Rest is essential as altitude begins to increase more rapidly from here
- Notes: Chhomrong to Dovan: approximately 10 km, 4–5 hours. No alcohol or meat products are allowed beyond Chhomrong inside the Annapurna Sanctuary — this is an ACAP regulation. Teahouses become more basic but remain clean and functional. Watch for leeches on the trail in the bamboo sections even in October.

**2027-10-22**
- Morning: Trek from Dovan through the narrowing gorge past Himalaya Hotel (2,920m) and Hinku Cave (3,170m). The landscape transforms dramatically as the valley opens up. Pass through Deurali (3,230m) — the narrowest and most avalanche-prone section of the route. Move through this section briskly in clear weather
- Afternoon: Emerge from the gorge into the stunning Annapurna Base Camp (ABC) approach. The Sanctuary opens up with a breathtaking 360-degree amphitheater of Himalayan peaks. Arrive at Machhapuchhre Base Camp (MBC, 3,700m) and check in. Rest and acclimatize — do not rush to ABC today
- Evening: Watch the alpenglow on Machhapuchhre (Fishtail Peak, 6,993m) — one of the most sacred and unclimbed peaks in Nepal. The evening light on the peaks is extraordinary. Rest well for the big day tomorrow
- Notes: Dovan to MBC: approximately 11 km, 5–6 hours, significant altitude gain to 3,700m. Altitude sickness risk increases here — watch for persistent headache, loss of appetite, dizziness, or nausea. If symptoms are severe, descend immediately. Drink 4+ liters of water. Garlic soup is a local remedy for altitude. MBC teahouses are basic but adequate.

**2027-10-23**
- Morning: Rise early and trek the final 1.5–2 hours from MBC to Annapurna Base Camp (ABC, 4,130m). Arrive at ABC for sunrise — the full 360-degree panorama of Annapurna I (8,091m), Annapurna South, Hiunchuli, Gangapurna, Glacier Dome, Tent Peak, and Machhapuchhre is utterly overwhelming and magnificent
- Afternoon: Spend the morning and early afternoon at ABC exploring the glacier moraine, photographing the peaks, and absorbing the atmosphere of this extraordinary high-altitude sanctuary. Have lunch at the ABC teahouse. Begin descent back toward MBC in the afternoon — it is not advisable to spend the night at ABC unless fully acclimatized
- Evening: Return to MBC or descend further to Deurali or Himalaya Hotel depending on energy levels. Celebrate reaching Annapurna Base Camp with your trekking companions over dinner
- Notes: MBC to ABC: approximately 4 km, 1.5–2 hours. This is the highlight of the entire trip. October weather is generally clear and stable — ideal conditions. Temperatures at ABC can drop to -5°C to -10°C even in October mornings. Wear all your layers. Do not linger too long if you feel unwell at altitude. The descent from ABC back to lower elevations is the best remedy for altitude sickness.

**2027-10-24**
- Morning: Begin the descent from MBC (or Himalaya Hotel) back through the gorge, retracing the trail through Deurali, Hinku Cave, and Himalaya Hotel. The descent is faster but hard on the knees — use trekking poles. The familiar trail feels different going downhill with new perspectives
- Afternoon: Continue descending through Dovan and Bamboo, arriving back at Sinuwa or Chhomrong by mid-afternoon. The increasing warmth and lush vegetation as you lose altitude is a welcome change. Arrive at Chhomrong and enjoy a well-deserved rest
- Evening: Celebrate the successful ABC trek with fellow trekkers in Chhomrong. Hot shower, clean clothes, and a proper meal feel like luxury after the high-altitude teahouses
- Notes: Descent day: approximately 14–16 km, 6–7 hours. Knees take significant strain on long descents — take breaks, use poles, and step carefully on wet stones. You have now completed the core Annapurna Sanctuary trek. The next days will offer a more relaxed pace with cultural exploration on the return route.

**2027-10-25**
- Morning: Trek from Chhomrong to Jhinu Danda (1,780m) — a shorter, easier day. The trail descends through terraced fields and forest. Jhinu Danda is famous for its natural hot springs by the Modi Khola river
- Afternoon: Spend the afternoon soaking in the natural hot springs at Jhinu Danda — a perfect remedy for tired trekking muscles. The springs are set beside the rushing river with mountain views. This is one of the most rewarding rest stops on the entire circuit
- Evening: Return up to Jhinu Danda village for the evening. Enjoy a relaxed dinner and early night. The hot springs will leave you deeply relaxed and refreshed
- Notes: Chhomrong to Jhinu Danda: approximately 5 km, 2 hours. This is a deliberate rest and recovery day — do not rush. The hot springs cost approximately NPR 200–300 entry. Bring a towel and swimwear. This detour via Jhinu is highly recommended and adds minimal extra distance to the return route.

**2027-10-26**
- Morning: Trek from Jhinu Danda back toward Nayapul via Ghandruk (1,940m) — the largest Gurung village in the Annapurna region. The trail climbs from Jhinu through forest and terraced fields with excellent views of Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre
- Afternoon: Arrive at Ghandruk and explore this beautifully preserved Gurung village. Visit the Gurung Museum to learn about the indigenous culture, traditional dress, and the famous Gurkha military heritage. Walk the stone-paved lanes and interact with local villagers
- Evening: Stay overnight in Ghandruk — one of the most atmospheric villages on the trek. Enjoy cultural performances if available at the teahouse. The village has excellent views for sunset over the Annapurna range
- Notes: Jhinu Danda to Ghandruk: approximately 7 km, 3–4 hours. Ghandruk is a culturally rich village and a highlight of the return journey. The Gurung Museum (ACAP-run) is excellent and free with your conservation area permit. This is the best place to buy authentic local crafts and souvenirs.

**2027-10-27**
- Morning: Final morning in Ghandruk — wake up for sunrise views of the Annapurna range from the village. Take a short walk to the upper viewpoint above Ghandruk for a last look at the peaks. Then begin the descent to Kimche and onward to Nayapul via Birethanti
- Afternoon: Arrive at Nayapul and catch a local bus or jeep back to Pokhara (approximately 1.5–2 hours). Check back in to your Pokhara lakeside guesthouse, drop your pack, and take a long hot shower. Spend the afternoon at leisure — visit a spa for a traditional Nepali massage, browse the lakeside market, or simply relax by Phewa Lake
- Evening: Celebratory dinner in Pokhara to mark the completion of the Annapurna Sanctuary Trek. Enjoy a proper restaurant meal with a wider menu choice after days of teahouse food
- Notes: Ghandruk to Nayapul: approximately 10 km, 3–4 hours. You have now completed the full Annapurna Sanctuary circuit. Total trekking distance approximately 110–120 km over 11 days. Get a massage this evening — your legs deserve it. Confirm your return transport to Kathmandu for tomorrow.

**2027-10-28**
- Morning: Final morning in Pokhara — wake up for a last sunrise view of the Annapurna range from Lakeside or take a short walk to the Peace Pagoda for a final panoramic farewell to the mountains. Visit the Seti River Gorge viewpoint in central Pokhara — a dramatic natural wonder often overlooked by trekkers
- Afternoon: Transfer back to Kathmandu by tourist bus (6–7 hours) or domestic flight (25 minutes). If time allows before departure, visit Thamel in Kathmandu for last-minute souvenir shopping — thangka paintings, singing bowls, pashmina scarves, and trekking gear at good prices. Visit Boudhanath Stupa or Pashupatinath Temple if arriving with sufficient time
- Evening: Final evening in Nepal — enjoy a farewell dinner in Kathmandu's Thamel district. Reflect on the extraordinary 14-day journey through one of the world's most spectacular mountain regions
- Notes: This is your final day — keep it flexible based on your onward flight time. If departing late or the following day, Kathmandu has excellent museums (National Museum, Natural History Museum) and the Durbar Squares are worth visiting. Keep some Nepali Rupees for airport departure tax and last-minute purchases. Reflect on an incredible 14 days in the Himalayas — you have trekked to Annapurna Base Camp at 4,130m, experienced authentic Gurung and Magar village culture, and witnessed some of the most spectacular mountain scenery on Earth.
