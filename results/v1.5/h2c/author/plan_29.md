## Day by day

**2027-11-15 (Monday)**
- Morning: No activities scheduled — flight arrives at 13:00. Rest and freshen up after landing.
- Afternoon: Check in to your boutique stay near Angkor Archaeological Park. Take a gentle orientation stroll around the hotel grounds and nearby Siem Reap Old Market (Phsar Chas) area to get your bearings, pick up a local SIM card, and exchange currency.
- Evening: Explore Pub Street and the surrounding night market on foot. Browse local handicrafts and silk scarves at the Angkor Night Market. Enjoy a relaxed first evening soaking in the atmosphere.
- Notes: Do not over-schedule Day 1. Jet lag and travel fatigue are real. Purchase a 3-day or 7-day Angkor Pass at the official ticket office (open until 17:30) if you plan to visit the temples starting Day 2 — it saves time in the morning queue.

**2027-11-16 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Early rise for sunrise at Angkor Wat (arrive by 05:30). Watch the iconic reflection of the temple towers in the lotus pond as the sun rises — one of Southeast Asia's most memorable sights. Explore the outer galleries and bas-reliefs depicting the Churning of the Ocean of Milk.
- Afternoon: Continue to Angkor Thom complex: visit the Bayon Temple (famous for its 216 giant stone faces), the Baphuon, Phimeanakas, and the Terrace of the Elephants. Take a tuk-tuk between sites (~$15–20 for a full-day driver).
- Evening: Return to Siem Reap town. Attend a traditional Apsara dance performance at Koulen II Restaurant or Smile of Angkor — a cultural highlight showcasing ancient Khmer court dance. Dinner included at the venue.
- Notes: November weather in Siem Reap is ideal — dry season begins, temperatures around 28–32°C. Wear light, breathable clothing and bring sunscreen and a hat. Stay hydrated. Angkor Pass is required — buy it the evening before (Day 1) to maximize morning time.

**2027-11-17 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit Ta Prohm — the jungle temple famously left partially unrestored, with massive strangler fig and silk-cotton trees growing through the ruins (made famous by Tomb Raider). Arrive early (~07:00) before tour groups arrive for a more atmospheric experience.
- Afternoon: Head to Banteay Srei, located about 25 km northeast of Siem Reap. Known as the 'Citadel of Women,' this 10th-century temple is celebrated for its intricate pink sandstone carvings — considered the finest in all of Angkor. En route, stop at the Landmine Museum to learn about Cambodia's history with UXO and ongoing demining efforts.
- Evening: Back in Siem Reap, visit the Angkor National Museum for a deeper understanding of Khmer civilization, artifacts, and the history of the Angkor Empire. Open until 18:30. (~$12 entry fee)
- Notes: Hire a tuk-tuk for the full day to cover the distance to Banteay Srei comfortably (~$20–25). The Landmine Museum is a sobering but important cultural stop — donations appreciated. Banteay Srei is not covered by the standard Angkor Pass — check current ticketing rules.

**2027-11-18 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit Preah Khan — a large flat temple complex built by Jayavarman VII, less crowded than Angkor Wat but equally impressive, with long corridors, carved doorways, and atmospheric tree roots. Then walk to nearby Neak Pean, a small island temple set in an artificial pond symbolizing the Himalayan lake Anavatapta.
- Afternoon: Take a boat trip on Tonlé Sap Lake — the largest freshwater lake in Southeast Asia. Visit a floating village (Kampong Phluk is more authentic and less touristy than Chong Kneas). See stilted houses, floating schools, and local life on the water. (~$15–20 for boat tour)
- Evening: Free evening in Siem Reap. Explore Kandal Village — a quieter, more boutique neighborhood with independent cafés, art galleries, and design shops. Great for a relaxed walk and souvenir shopping.
- Notes: November is a great time to visit Tonlé Sap — the lake is at or near its maximum size after the monsoon season, making floating village access easier. Bring insect repellent for the lake excursion. Wear shoes you don't mind getting wet.

**2027-11-19 (Friday)**
- Morning: Depart Siem Reap by morning bus or private transfer to Phnom Penh (~6 hours by bus, ~$12–15 via Giant Ibis or Mekong Express, or ~$80–100 by private car). Enjoy the countryside scenery along the way.
- Afternoon: Arrive in Phnom Penh early-to-mid afternoon. Check in to your guesthouse or mid-range hotel near the Riverside (Sisowath Quay area recommended). Take a walk along the Mekong Riverside promenade to stretch your legs and get oriented.
- Evening: Visit the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda (check closing times — usually 17:00, may need to visit early next morning). If closed, stroll the palace exterior and Riverside at golden hour, then explore the Night Market along Sisowath Quay.
- Notes: Book your bus ticket from Siem Reap to Phnom Penh in advance, especially for premium operators like Giant Ibis which offer comfortable seats, WiFi, and reliability. Alternatively, a 45-minute domestic flight is available for ~$50–80 if you prefer speed. Accommodation in Phnom Penh not specified — book a mid-range hotel near Riverside for convenience.

**2027-11-20 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Visit the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda (open from 08:00). Admire the Throne Hall, the Moonlight Pavilion, and the Silver Pagoda housing the Emerald Buddha and a life-size gold Buddha encrusted with 9,584 diamonds. Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered. (~$10 entry)
- Afternoon: Visit the National Museum of Cambodia — a stunning terracotta building housing the world's finest collection of Khmer art, including statues from Angkor spanning centuries. (~$10 entry). Afterward, walk to the Central Market (Phsar Thmei) — a stunning Art Deco dome market for browsing jewelry, clothing, and souvenirs.
- Evening: Sunset cruise on the Mekong and Tonlé Sap confluence — a beautiful way to see Phnom Penh from the water as the city lights up. Many operators offer 1–1.5 hour cruises (~$10–15). Return for dinner in the Riverside area.
- Notes: Phnom Penh is more urban and traffic-heavy than Siem Reap. Use PassApp tuk-tuks (app-based, like Uber for tuk-tuks) for safe and fairly priced transport around the city. Dress codes apply at the Royal Palace.

**2027-11-21 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (S-21) — the former high school turned Khmer Rouge prison where an estimated 17,000 people were tortured and executed. A deeply sobering but essential historical experience for understanding Cambodia's recent past. Allow 2–3 hours. (~$8 entry, audio guide recommended)
- Afternoon: Take a half-day trip to the Killing Fields of Choeung Ek (~15 km from city center, ~$5 tuk-tuk ride). The memorial stupa and audio guide (included in $6 entry) provide a powerful and moving account of the genocide. Return to the city by mid-afternoon for some quiet reflection time.
- Evening: Lighter evening — browse the Russian Market (Phsar Tuol Tom Poung) for handicrafts, silk, and local goods before it closes (~17:00–18:00). Then enjoy a peaceful dinner and early night.
- Notes: The Tuol Sleng and Killing Fields visits are emotionally heavy. Give yourself time and space to process. Many travelers find it helpful to visit these sites together on one day and allow the evening to be calm and reflective. Not recommended for young children. Hire a guide for deeper historical context (~$10–15).

**2027-11-22 (Monday)**
- Morning: Depart Phnom Penh for Sihanoukville by bus (~4–5 hours, ~$10–12 via Giant Ibis or Cambodian Bus) or take a direct bus/van to Kep or Kampot (~3 hours, ~$8–10) — a more charming coastal alternative. Kampot is strongly recommended for a mid-range traveler seeking authentic beach and culture experiences.
- Afternoon: Arrive in Kampot. Check in to a mid-range guesthouse or boutique hotel along the Kampot River (many charming options for $30–60/night). Explore the charming French colonial Old Town on foot — colorful shop-houses, riverside promenade, and the iconic Durian Roundabout.
- Evening: Sunset kayaking or boat cruise on the Kampot River through mangroves — a magical experience as fireflies begin to glow at dusk. Many guesthouses and tour operators offer this for ~$10–15.
- Notes: Kampot is far preferable to Sihanoukville for mid-range travelers seeking culture and beaches — Sihanoukville has undergone significant overdevelopment. Kampot offers a relaxed, authentic atmosphere with excellent food, nearby beaches, and the famous Bokor National Park. Book accommodation in advance as good spots fill up.

**2027-11-23 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Kep — a small, laid-back seaside town 25 km from Kampot, famous for its crab market and the nearby Kep National Park. Rent a bicycle or take a tuk-tuk (~$10 return). Visit the Crab Market and browse the stalls of freshly caught seafood.
- Afternoon: Hike the trails of Kep National Park through jungle to viewpoints overlooking the Gulf of Thailand and Rabbit Island (Koh Tonsay). Alternatively, take a short boat to Rabbit Island itself (~$5 return) for a few hours on a beautiful, quiet beach with clear water and minimal development.
- Evening: Return to Kampot. Visit Kampot's famous pepper plantations — Kampot pepper is world-renowned and a UNESCO-recognized product. Many farms offer late afternoon tours (~$5–8). End the evening with a relaxed riverside walk.
- Notes: November weather on the coast is excellent — dry and sunny with calm seas. Perfect for beach and island visits. Bring reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, and water. The Kep crab lunch is a highlight not to be missed — arrive at the market between 11:00–13:00 for the freshest selection.

**2027-11-24 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Kampot — take a slow morning. Visit Phnom Chhngok Cave Temple (~8 km from Kampot) — a limestone cave hiding a small ancient Khmer temple inside, with beautiful stalactites and local spiritual significance. (~$1 entry, hire a local guide for $3–5).
- Afternoon: Return to Kampot town. Last-minute souvenir shopping — buy authentic Kampot pepper, locally made crafts, and silk items. Head to the bus station for an afternoon bus back to Phnom Penh (~3 hours) if flying out, or to Siem Reap if needed for a return flight.
- Evening: Depending on your departure logistics — if flying from Phnom Penh, arrive in the city by early evening, have a final Cambodian dinner near the airport or Riverside, and prepare for departure. If your flight is the following day, enjoy a final relaxed evening.
- Notes: Allow plenty of travel time on the final day. Phnom Penh International Airport is the main hub — arrive at least 2.5–3 hours before international departure. Confirm your return bus or transfer booking the night before. Consider buying extra Kampot pepper as gifts — it's lightweight, affordable, and a uniquely Cambodian souvenir. Safe travels!
