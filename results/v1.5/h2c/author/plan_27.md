## Day by day

**2027-03-01**
- Morning: Arrival at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Check in to your boutique hotel in Centro Histórico, freshen up, and get oriented in the neighborhood.
- Afternoon: Take a leisurely walk to the Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución), one of the largest city squares in the world. Explore the exterior of the Metropolitan Cathedral and admire the National Palace facade with its famous Diego Rivera murals visible from the courtyard. Stroll through the surrounding streets to soak in the colonial architecture.
- Evening: Head to the Templo Mayor archaeological site area for a golden-hour atmosphere, then explore the pedestrian street Madero. Wind down with a mezcal tasting at a local cantina near the hotel.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light since you arrive at 13:00. Focus on getting comfortable in the neighborhood. Confirm hotel check-in time in advance. Exchange some currency or use ATMs near the Zócalo.

**2027-03-02**
- Morning: Visit the Templo Mayor Museum (opens 9:00 AM) — the excavated Aztec temple complex right in the heart of Centro Histórico. Spend 1.5–2 hours exploring the ruins and the excellent museum showcasing pre-Hispanic artifacts.
- Afternoon: Walk to the Palacio de Bellas Artes to admire its stunning Art Nouveau and Art Deco architecture and view the Diego Rivera, Orozco, and Siqueiros murals inside. Afterward, explore the nearby Alameda Central park for a relaxing stroll among locals.
- Evening: Head to the Barrio Chino (Chinatown) area or explore the Correo Mayor street. Visit the Palacio Postal (Postal Palace) if still open, then enjoy a vibrant evening in a nearby pulquería for a traditional pulque experience.
- Notes: Templo Mayor is closed on Mondays. Bellas Artes hosts ballet folklórico performances on Sundays and Wednesdays — check the schedule and book tickets in advance if interested (~$15–25 USD).

**2027-03-03**
- Morning: Day trip to Teotihuacán — take an early bus from Terminal Norte (accessible by Metro from Centro) for about a 1-hour ride. Spend the morning climbing the Pyramid of the Sun and Pyramid of the Moon and walking the Avenue of the Dead. Arrive early to beat crowds and heat.
- Afternoon: Return to Mexico City by early afternoon. Rest briefly at the hotel, then visit the Museo Nacional de Antropología in Chapultepec Park (take the Metro to Auditorio or Chapultepec station) — one of the world's greatest anthropology museums housing the Aztec Sun Stone.
- Evening: Explore the trendy Condesa or Roma Norte neighborhood for an evening stroll. Browse boutique shops and enjoy the Art Deco architecture before dinner.
- Notes: Wear comfortable shoes and sunscreen for Teotihuacán — it involves significant walking and climbing. Bring water. Buses to Teotihuacán depart every 30 minutes from Terminal Norte. The site opens at 9:00 AM.

**2027-03-04**
- Morning: Visit Coyoacán neighborhood — take the Metro (Line 3 to Viveros or Coyoacán station). Explore the charming colonial streets, the main plaza (Jardín Centenario), and the iconic Frida Kahlo Museum (La Casa Azul). Book tickets online in advance as they sell out quickly.
- Afternoon: Walk to the nearby Leon Trotsky Museum (Casa de Trotsky) for a fascinating historical detour. Then browse the Mercado de Artesanías de Coyoacán for unique local crafts, textiles, and souvenirs at mid-range prices.
- Evening: Return to Centro Histórico. Explore the nightlife around República de Uruguay and Regina streets — a lively pedestrian zone with bars, live music, and street food vendors that comes alive after dark.
- Notes: Frida Kahlo Museum tickets must be booked well in advance online at museofridakahlo.org.mx — walk-in tickets are rarely available. Coyoacán is very walkable once you arrive. The neighborhood is especially lively on weekends.

**2027-03-05**
- Morning: Visit the Mercado de la Merced or Mercado Jamaica for an immersive local market experience — one of the largest traditional markets in Latin America. Watch vendors, sample street food, and observe everyday Mexico City life. Then stop by the nearby Museo de la Ciudad de México (City Museum) for a quick cultural overview.
- Afternoon: Return to the Zócalo area for any last-minute sights you may have missed — consider the interior of the National Palace to see Diego Rivera's epic murals up close (free entry with ID). Visit a local chocolate or artisan shop on Calle Mesones for edible souvenirs.
- Evening: Farewell dinner in Centro Histórico. Pack your bags and enjoy a relaxed final evening.
- Notes: Check your departure flight time and arrange airport transfer in advance. Mexico City traffic can be heavy — allow at least 1.5–2 hours to reach the airport during peak hours. Keep some pesos for tips and last-minute purchases.
