## Day by day

**2027-12-15 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: No activities scheduled — flight arrives at 13:00
- Afternoon: Arrive at Incheon International Airport at 13:00. Clear immigration, collect baggage, and take the AREX Express Train (~43 min) to Seoul Station. Check in to your Boutique Stay in Jongno-gu (approx. 15:00–15:30). Freshen up and take a gentle orientation stroll around Insadong alley — browse antique shops, traditional crafts, and street art to get your first feel of Seoul.
- Evening: Walk to Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천) for a relaxing evening stroll along the illuminated waterway. The winter light festival decorations make it especially magical in December. Wind down nearby.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light — jet lag is real. Exchange some currency at the airport (KEB Hana Bank counter) or use a Wise/Revolut card. Pick up a T-money card at any convenience store for seamless subway travel.

**2027-12-16 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Start with Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) — arrive early (~09:00) to watch the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony (held at 10:00 and 14:00). Rent a hanbok at a nearby rental shop (₩15,000–20,000/2hrs) for free palace entry and stunning photos against the traditional architecture and snow-dusted rooftops.
- Afternoon: Walk north to Bukchon Hanok Village (북촌 한옥마을) — wander the narrow lanes lined with traditional Korean houses. Then explore Samcheongdong-gil (삼청동길), a charming street full of independent galleries, cafés, and boutiques. Visit the National Folk Museum of Korea inside the palace grounds (~₩3,000).
- Evening: Head to Gwanghwamun Square (광화문광장) to see the Yi Sun-sin statue and seasonal winter installations. Explore the nearby underground Gwanghwamun shopping arcade for warmth and local browsing.
- Notes: Gyeongbokgung is closed on Tuesdays — Wednesday is perfect. Wear warm layers; December temperatures in Seoul range from -5°C to 5°C. The hanbok experience is highly recommended for photos.

**2027-12-17 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit Changdeokgung Palace (창덕궁) and its UNESCO-listed Secret Garden (후원 — Huwon). Book the Secret Garden tour in advance online (~₩8,000). The frozen pond and bare winter trees create an ethereal atmosphere. Nearby Changgyeonggung Palace can be explored as a bonus.
- Afternoon: Explore Ikseon-dong Hanok Village (익선동) — Seoul's trendiest retro neighborhood with hanok buildings converted into indie cafés, vintage clothing stores, and fusion restaurants. Very close to your Jongno-gu hotel. Browse at a leisurely pace.
- Evening: Visit Jongmyo Shrine (종묘) — a serene UNESCO World Heritage Site, the royal ancestral shrine of the Joseon Dynasty. Then walk along Jongno (종로) street for evening energy and neon lights.
- Notes: Ikseon-dong is extremely photogenic and very close to your accommodation — ideal for a relaxed afternoon. Book Changdeokgung Secret Garden online at heritage.go.kr as spots are limited.

**2027-12-18 (Friday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Namsangol Hanok Village (남산골 한옥마을) at the base of Namsan Mountain — a free open-air museum of restored traditional homes with winter cultural performances. Then take the Namsan Cable Car (~₩12,000 round trip) up to N Seoul Tower (남산타워).
- Afternoon: Explore N Seoul Tower observation deck (~₩21,000) for panoramic views of the city. Browse the love lock fence and the digital art installations inside. Descend via the scenic walking trail (Namsan둘레길) if weather permits — about 40 minutes downhill through pine forest.
- Evening: Head to Myeongdong (명동) — Seoul's famous shopping and street food district. Explore the outdoor market stalls selling hotteok, egg bread, lobster skewers, and tornado potatoes. Great for skincare shopping at Innisfree, Etude House, and The Face Shop.
- Notes: Myeongdong is extremely popular with tourists but the street food and skincare shopping are genuinely excellent. Go on a Friday evening for peak atmosphere. N Seoul Tower is beautiful at night.

**2027-12-19 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Explore Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP — 동대문디자인플라자) — Zaha Hadid's futuristic landmark. Check the current exhibitions (usually ₩5,000–10,000). The surrounding Dongdaemun History & Culture Park has remnants of the old Seoul city wall.
- Afternoon: Walk the Naksan Park (낙산공원) trail — a hillside park following the ancient Seoul fortress wall with great city views and street murals. Descend into Ihwa-dong Mural Village (이화동 벽화마을) for colorful public art and indie cafés.
- Evening: Explore Dongdaemun Fashion Town — the massive 24-hour wholesale and retail fashion market complex (Doota, Migliore, Hello apM). Saturdays are especially lively. Great for affordable Korean fashion finds.
- Notes: DDP is open late and the exterior is stunning when lit up at night. Saturday evening at Dongdaemun is extremely energetic. Wear comfortable walking shoes — Naksan trail involves steps and hills.

**2027-12-20 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit Jogyesa Temple (조계사) — the head temple of Korean Buddhism, located right in Jongno-gu. In December it's decorated with thousands of lanterns for winter festivals. Attend a morning bell ceremony if available. Then visit the nearby Buddhist cultural center.
- Afternoon: Explore the Sejong Center for the Performing Arts (세종문화회관) area and check for any Sunday afternoon cultural performances or exhibitions. Walk along the Cheonggyecheon Stream again in daylight for a different perspective, heading east toward the more local sections.
- Evening: Visit Insadong (인사동) for a Sunday evening market — local artisans, traditional crafts, and street performers. Browse Ssamziegil (쌈지길), an indie courtyard mall with unique Korean designer goods.
- Notes: Jogyesa Temple is free to enter and deeply peaceful — a wonderful contrast to the busy shopping areas. Insadong Sunday market is one of the best in Seoul for authentic crafts and K-culture souvenirs.

**2027-12-21 (Monday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Bukhansan National Park (북한산국립공원) — take the subway to Gupabal or Dobongsan station. Choose a beginner-friendly trail like the Bukhansanseong Fortress trail (~2–3 hours) for stunning winter mountain views and ancient fortress walls dusted with snow.
- Afternoon: Return to the city by early afternoon. Visit Seodaemun Prison History Hall (서대문형무소역사관) — a sobering and important museum documenting Korea's independence movement and Japanese colonial period (~₩3,000). Essential for understanding Korean history and culture.
- Evening: Explore Hongdae (홍대) neighborhood — Seoul's university arts district, famous for street performances, indie music venues, and youthful energy. Walk the main street and duck into vintage shops and K-indie music bars.
- Notes: Bukhansan in winter requires good footwear — trail crampons (아이젠, ice grippers) can be rented at the trailhead for ~₩3,000. Check trail conditions in advance. Seodaemun closes on Mondays — swap with Tuesday if needed.

**2027-12-22 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Explore the National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관) in Yongsan — one of Asia's largest museums, with an extraordinary collection of Korean artifacts from prehistoric times through the Joseon Dynasty. Free general admission. Allow 3 hours.
- Afternoon: Walk or take the subway to Itaewon (이태원) — Seoul's most international neighborhood. Visit the Leeum Samsung Museum of Art (리움미술관) for world-class Korean and contemporary art (~₩20,000). Browse Antique Furniture Street and the Haebangchon (HBC) hillside neighborhood.
- Evening: Stay in Itaewon for the evening — explore the main strip and side streets. Visit a rooftop bar for December skyline views with a warm drink.
- Notes: The National Museum of Korea is genuinely world-class and completely free for the permanent collection — do not skip it. Leeum Museum has exceptional Korean celadon and contemporary Korean art.

**2027-12-23 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit Gyeonghuigung Palace (경희궁) — a lesser-visited palace near Gwanghwamun, peaceful and crowd-free. Then walk to the Seoul Museum of History (서울역사박물관) next door — free admission, excellent exhibits on Seoul's transformation from Joseon capital to modern megacity.
- Afternoon: Explore Seochon Village (서촌마을) — a charming neighborhood west of Gyeongbokgung, home to artists, writers, and indie cafés in traditional alleyways. Visit Yun Dong-ju Literature House (윤동주문학관) — a beautifully converted water tank honoring Korea's beloved resistance poet.
- Evening: Pre-Christmas Eve atmosphere in Gwanghwamun and Jongno — Seoul decorates extensively for Christmas. Enjoy the festive lights, street performances, and seasonal markets around City Hall Plaza (서울광장) where an outdoor ice rink is typically set up.
- Notes: Tongin Market dosirak experience is one of Seoul's most unique food experiences — highly recommended. Seoul City Hall ice rink is open in December and skate rentals are available (~₩1,000 entry + ₩2,000 rental).

**2027-12-24 (Thursday — Christmas Eve)**
- Morning: Visit the Korean Folk Village (한국민속촌) in Yongin — about 1 hour from Seoul by subway + shuttle. This living open-air museum recreates traditional Korean village life with performances, craftspeople, and seasonal winter activities. Special Christmas-themed Korean cultural events often held.
- Afternoon: Return to Seoul by early afternoon. Explore Lotte World (롯데월드) in Jamsil — Asia's largest indoor theme park with an outdoor section. On Christmas Eve it's beautifully decorated. Alternatively, visit the adjacent Lotte World Tower Sky Observatory (롯데월드타워 서울스카이, ~₩29,000) for the highest views in Korea.
- Evening: Christmas Eve in Seoul is a major event — Koreans celebrate it as a romantic holiday. Join the festivities in Myeongdong or Gangnam. Attend a candlelight service at Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당) — Seoul's most iconic Catholic church, stunning in December.
- Notes: Christmas Eve is one of Seoul's busiest nights — book dinner in advance. Myeongdong Cathedral's midnight mass is beautiful but very crowded. Lotte World Tower Sky Observatory offers extraordinary nighttime city views.

**2027-12-25 (Friday — Christmas Day)**
- Morning: Christmas morning — visit Deoksugung Palace (덕수궁) in the heart of the city. The stone wall road (돌담길) around the palace is one of Seoul's most romantic walks. Watch the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony in traditional winter dress. The adjacent Seoul Museum of Art (서울시립미술관) has free exhibitions.
- Afternoon: Explore Gangnam (강남) district — visit COEX Mall (코엑스몰), one of Asia's largest underground malls, including the spectacular Starfield Library (별마당 도서관). Browse the Bongeunsa Temple (봉은사) adjacent to COEX — a 1,200-year-old temple dramatically contrasted against Gangnam skyscrapers.
- Evening: Experience Gangnam's upscale side — stroll Garosu-gil (가로수길), a tree-lined boulevard of boutique fashion stores, galleries, and restaurants. Christmas evening atmosphere with lights and decorations.
- Notes: Many smaller shops may be closed on Christmas Day but major attractions, malls, and restaurants are open. Bongeunsa Temple on Christmas Day offers a beautiful spiritual contrast. Gangnam subway station is a major hub — very accessible from Jongno-gu.

**2027-12-26 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Explore the War Memorial of Korea (전쟁기념관) in Yongsan — a comprehensive and moving museum covering Korean military history, with particular focus on the Korean War. Free admission. The outdoor sculpture garden features tanks, planes, and monuments.
- Afternoon: Visit Noryangjin Fish Market (노량진수산시장) — Seoul's famous wholesale fish market. Buy fresh seafood from the market stalls and take it upstairs to a restaurant that will prepare it for you (hoe — Korean-style raw fish). An authentic and memorable experience.
- Evening: Final evening exploration of Jongno-gu — your home neighborhood. Walk Pimatgol Alley (피맛골), a historic back alley with old-school Korean restaurants. Visit a norebang (노래방 — private karaoke room) for a quintessential K-culture experience (~₩15,000–20,000/hour for a room).
- Notes: Noryangjin Fish Market is best visited for lunch — the freshest catch is available in the morning. Norebang is a must-do K-culture experience — groups of any size can book a private room. Great for a final Saturday night in Seoul.

**2027-12-27 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Seoul — revisit your favorite neighborhood, Jongno-gu/Insadong, for last-minute souvenir shopping. Best souvenirs: Korean tea sets, hanji (traditional paper) crafts, gochujang paste sets, Korean beauty products, and traditional ceramics. Visit Namdaemun Market (남대문시장) nearby for wholesale prices on Korean goods.
- Afternoon: Depending on departure flight time — if afternoon/evening departure: take the AREX Express from Seoul Station to Incheon Airport. Allow 3 hours before departure for check-in and security. If late departure: visit Cheonggyecheon Stream one final time for a reflective farewell walk along the waterway that welcomed you on Day 1.
- Evening: Depart from Incheon International Airport. Final Korean meal at the airport — Bibigo restaurant in the departure terminal for a proper bibimbap or bulgogi set as a last taste of Seoul (~₩15,000–20,000).
- Notes: AREX Express to Incheon takes 43 minutes from Seoul Station. Return your T-money card at the airport for a refund of remaining balance (minus ₩500 handling fee). Most major Korean airlines and shops at Incheon accept credit cards. Pack Korean snacks (choco pie, honey butter chips, dried seaweed) for the flight home!
