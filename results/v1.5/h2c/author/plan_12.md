## Day by day

**2027-12-15 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Arrival at Incheon International Airport at 13:00 — clear customs, collect luggage, and take the AREX Express Train (~43 min) to Seoul Station, then transfer to Gyeongbokgung area. Check in to your boutique stay in Jongno-gu and freshen up.
- Afternoon: Take a gentle orientation stroll around Gyeongbokgung Palace's outer walls and Gyeongbokgung Station area. Walk through the charming Bukchon Hanok Village entrance streets to get your bearings. Visit a nearby convenience store (GS25 or CU) to grab Korean snacks and drinks for the room.
- Evening: Head to Insadong Street (10-min walk) for your first evening in Seoul. Browse the traditional craft shops, lantern-lit alleys, and Ssamziegil courtyard. Enjoy a relaxed dinner at a nearby Korean restaurant.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light due to jet lag. Exchange some currency at Incheon Airport (rates are competitive there). Buy a T-Money card at the airport for seamless subway and bus travel throughout the trip.

**2027-12-16 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit Gyeongbokgung Palace (opens 9:00, ~₩3,000 entry). Watch the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony at the main gate (10:00 & 14:00). Rent a Hanbok from a nearby rental shop (₩15,000–25,000/hour) for free entry and beautiful photos inside the palace grounds.
- Afternoon: Walk to the National Folk Museum of Korea inside the palace grounds (free entry). Then explore Bukchon Hanok Village's upper alleys — wander the traditional tile-roofed hanok lanes with views over the city. Visit Gahoe-dong for the best photo spots.
- Evening: Head to Samcheong-dong for dinner and a relaxed evening walk. Browse the indie boutiques, galleries, and cafés along this trendy but traditional street.
- Notes: December is cold in Seoul (avg 0–5°C). Dress in warm layers. The Hanbok rental is highly recommended for the palace visit — many shops open by 9:30 AM near the palace entrance.

**2027-12-17 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit Changdeokgung Palace and its UNESCO-listed Secret Garden (Huwon). Book the Secret Garden guided tour in advance online (₩8,000, tours run at set times). The winter bare trees create a hauntingly beautiful atmosphere.
- Afternoon: Walk to Changgyeonggung Palace next door (₩1,000 entry) and then stroll through Ikseon-dong Hanok Village — a hip, renovated hanok alley filled with indie cafés, vintage shops, and small restaurants.
- Evening: Explore Jongno 3-ga area and visit Pimatgol Alley, a historic back-alley street lined with pojangmacha (street food tents) and traditional eateries. Perfect for a lively K-culture evening.
- Notes: Book the Changdeokgung Secret Garden tour at least a day in advance via the official website. Ikseon-dong is best visited in the afternoon when shops open around noon.

**2027-12-18 (Friday)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Gwangjang Market (Jongno 5-ga station) — one of Seoul's oldest and most authentic traditional markets. Explore the fabric stalls and dive into the famous food alley for a hearty market breakfast.
- Afternoon: Head to Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP) — the futuristic landmark designed by Zaha Hadid. Explore the design exhibitions inside (free to walk the exterior, exhibitions vary ₩5,000–10,000). Browse the surrounding Dongdaemun shopping district.
- Evening: Dongdaemun market district comes alive at night. Visit the outdoor night market stalls and the massive multi-story fashion malls (Doota, Migliore). Great for K-fashion browsing and street food.
- Notes: Gwangjang Market is most lively in the morning. Dongdaemun's fashion malls typically open from noon and run until very late (some until 5 AM). Friday night will be especially vibrant.

**2027-12-19 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Bukhansan National Park (40 min by subway + bus). Hike the Bukhansanseong Fortress Trail — a manageable winter hike with stunning views of Seoul from ancient fortress walls. Wear proper footwear as paths may be icy.
- Afternoon: Return to the city and visit Seochon Village (서촌) — a quieter, more local alternative to Bukchon. Explore the narrow alleyways, independent bookshops, and art studios near Gyeongbokgung's west side.
- Evening: Relax at a traditional Korean Jjimjilbang (sauna/bathhouse) near Jongno — Dragon Hill Spa is a popular option (₩12,000–15,000 entry). Perfect for warming up after a cold day of hiking.
- Notes: Check trail conditions before heading to Bukhansan in December — some upper trails may be closed due to ice. Bring hand warmers, crampons if possible, and water. The jjimjilbang is a quintessential K-culture experience — try the sauna rooms, hot pools, and sleeping area.

**2027-12-20 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit Jogyesa Temple (10-min walk from hotel) — the head temple of Korean Buddhism in Seoul. December may feature beautiful lantern decorations. Attend a morning ceremony if interested (free entry). Walk through the Insa-dong antique and tea district nearby.
- Afternoon: Explore the National Museum of Korea in Yongsan (free entry, subway accessible). One of Asia's largest museums with incredible Korean artifacts, celadon pottery, and Buddhist art. Spend 2–3 hours inside.
- Evening: Head to Itaewon for a multicultural evening — browse the eclectic shops, international restaurants, and K-culture boutiques. Visit Gyeongnidan-gil for a trendier, less touristy experience.
- Notes: Jogyesa Temple is free to enter and particularly atmospheric in the early morning. The National Museum is closed on Mondays, so Sunday is ideal. Itaewon is very accessible and offers a great mix of international and Korean culture.

**2027-12-21 (Monday)**
- Morning: Visit Namsan Mountain and N Seoul Tower. Take the cable car up (₩9,500 round trip) or hike the trail (30–40 min). Enjoy panoramic winter views of Seoul. See the famous love lock fence and the tower exterior.
- Afternoon: Walk down Namsan toward Myeongdong — Seoul's most famous shopping and street food district. Browse K-beauty shops (Innisfree, Etude House, Olive Young), skincare, and cosmetics. Great for affordable K-beauty shopping.
- Evening: Stay in Myeongdong for the evening street food market — one of the best in Seoul. The street vendors set up as the sun goes down with incredible variety.
- Notes: Monday is a good day for Myeongdong as weekends can be overwhelmingly crowded. Dress very warmly for Namsan — it's significantly colder and windier at elevation. The N Seoul Tower observation deck costs extra (₩21,000) but the exterior view is free.

**2027-12-22 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Hongdae (Hongik University area) — Seoul's youth culture, indie music, and street art hub. Explore the murals, independent boutiques, and record shops. Visit the Hongdae Free Market if operating (usually weekends, but street art is always present).
- Afternoon: Walk or take a short taxi to Sinchon and then Yeonnam-dong — a charming neighborhood with a converted railway park (Gyeongui Line Forest Park), indie cafés, and a relaxed creative atmosphere. Great for an afternoon wander.
- Evening: Return to Hongdae for the nightlife and live music scene. Check out a K-indie live music venue (Club FF, Cafe Bbang) or busking performances near the main plaza.
- Notes: Hongdae is one of the best areas to experience modern K-culture, K-pop influence, and youth street culture. Many idol agency buildings are nearby (HYBE, JYP). Look for impromptu busking performances especially in the evening.

**2027-12-23 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit Deoksugung Palace (₩1,000 entry) near City Hall — the only palace with a Western-style stone hall. Watch the unique Royal Guard Changing Ceremony here (10:30 & 14:30). Walk the Deoksugung Stone Wall Road (Doldam-gil), one of Seoul's most romantic winter walks.
- Afternoon: Explore the Seoul Museum of History (free entry) near Gyeongbokgung. Then visit Cheonggyecheon Stream — a restored urban stream running through central Seoul. Walk along the illuminated stream path.
- Evening: Head to Cheonggyecheon's Christmas illuminations (the stream is beautifully lit in December) and then explore the nearby Jongno Tower area. Visit a rooftop bar or café for evening city views.
- Notes: Cheonggyecheon is especially magical in December with Christmas and winter light festivals. The Deoksugung Stone Wall Road is a beloved Seoul walking spot — take your time and enjoy the atmosphere.

**2027-12-24 (Thursday — Christmas Eve)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Gangnam district — explore the upscale Apgujeong and Cheongdam-dong areas, known as the heart of K-pop culture. Visit SM Entertainment's SM Town COEX Artium for K-pop merchandise and interactive exhibits.
- Afternoon: Visit COEX Mall — one of Asia's largest underground malls. Explore the famous Starfield Library (free, Instagram-famous), the aquarium (optional, ₩29,000), and browse the K-pop and K-culture shops.
- Evening: Christmas Eve in Seoul — Myeongdong Cathedral hosts special Christmas masses and the area is beautifully decorated. Alternatively, enjoy the festive atmosphere along Cheonggyecheon or a Christmas dinner at a special restaurant.
- Notes: Christmas Eve in Seoul is festive and romantic. The city is beautifully decorated. Myeongdong Cathedral's midnight mass is a notable experience. Book dinner in advance as popular restaurants fill up quickly on Christmas Eve.

**2027-12-25 (Friday — Christmas Day)**
- Morning: Christmas Day in Seoul — visit Gyeongbokgung Palace again for a peaceful morning walk (many locals visit on holidays). Alternatively, explore the quieter Jongmyo Shrine (UNESCO, ₩1,000) — the royal ancestral shrine, serene and culturally significant.
- Afternoon: Visit the National Palace Museum of Korea (free, inside Gyeongbokgung grounds) for deeper cultural immersion. Then relax at a traditional Korean tea house in Insadong — try Dawon Tea House inside Unhyeongung Palace garden.
- Evening: Celebrate Christmas Korean-style — enjoy a festive street food walk through Insadong or Myeongdong, then find a cozy pojangmacha tent for warmth and Korean street snacks with locals.
- Notes: Christmas is a public holiday in South Korea and widely celebrated, especially among young Koreans. Many shops and restaurants are open. Strawberry cream cake is the traditional Korean Christmas dessert — pick one up from a local bakery!

**2027-12-26 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Take a day trip to Suwon Hwaseong Fortress (1 hour by subway from Seoul) — a magnificent UNESCO-listed fortress from the Joseon Dynasty. Walk the full fortress wall circuit (about 5.7 km) with stunning winter views.
- Afternoon: Explore Suwon's traditional Paldalmun Market near the fortress — one of Korea's most authentic traditional markets. Browse local food stalls and crafts. Return to Seoul by late afternoon.
- Evening: Final full evening in Seoul — revisit your favorite neighborhood (Insadong, Hongdae, or Myeongdong) for last-minute shopping, souvenirs, and a memorable farewell dinner.
- Notes: Suwon Hwaseong is very manageable as a day trip. The fortress is especially beautiful in winter with snow or frost on the walls. Wear very warm clothes and good walking shoes. The Suwon Galbi is a must-try regional specialty.

**2027-12-27 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Seoul — take a slow farewell walk around Gyeongbokgung and your Jongno neighborhood. Visit any last spots you missed, pick up final souvenirs at Insadong, or browse a local Sunday market.
- Afternoon: Check out of hotel and make your way to Incheon Airport. Take the AREX Express from Seoul Station (43 min, ₩9,500) or a direct airport limousine bus from Jongno area. Arrive at the airport with ample time for check-in and departure.
- Evening: Departure from Incheon International Airport. Browse the excellent duty-free shops for last Korean souvenirs — Korean cosmetics, snacks, ginseng, and traditional crafts.
- Notes: Allow at least 3 hours before your flight at Incheon Airport — it's large and check-in lines can be long, especially post-Christmas. Use any remaining Korean Won at the airport or exchange back to your home currency. Incheon Airport has excellent facilities including a Korean Cultural Experience Zone in Terminal 1 (free traditional activities).
