## Day by day

**2027-04-15 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: No morning activities — flight arrives at 13:00. Use transit time to settle in.
- Afternoon: Arrive at Narita/Haneda, clear customs, and take the airport express to central Tokyo. Check into your Tsukiji boutique hotel, freshen up, then take a gentle orientation walk through the Tsukiji Outer Market to get your bearings. Browse the stalls selling pickles, dried seafood, kitchenware, and street snacks as the afternoon vendors wind down.
- Evening: Ease into Tokyo with a relaxed dinner in Tsukiji. Explore the backstreets around Tsukiji Honganji temple, then stroll along the Sumida River waterfront for your first Tokyo night view. Early bedtime recommended to fight jet lag.
- Notes: Purchase a Suica or Pasmo IC card at the airport for seamless transit throughout the trip. Exchange some yen at the airport for convenience. April in Tokyo is cherry blossom season — expect crowds and beautiful scenery.

**2027-04-16 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Wake up early and visit Tsukiji Outer Market for a classic Tokyo breakfast experience. Watch tuna being sliced, sample tamagoyaki (sweet rolled egg), and sip green tea from market vendors. This is one of Tokyo's most authentic food culture moments.
- Afternoon: Head to nearby Hamarikyu Gardens — a stunning Edo-period garden with a tidal pond, just a 10-minute walk from Tsukiji. Take a traditional tea ceremony at the garden's teahouse overlooking the water (¥500–¥800). Then take a short ferry up the Sumida River to Asakusa, passing under historic bridges.
- Evening: Explore Asakusa's Senso-ji Temple at dusk when the lanterns glow and crowds thin. Browse Nakamise Shopping Street for traditional crafts and souvenirs. Dinner in Asakusa's local dining scene.
- Notes: Hamarikyu Gardens entry is ¥300. The Sumida River ferry (Tokyo Cruise) costs about ¥780 and offers great city views. Senso-ji is free to enter. Wear comfortable shoes for walking.

**2027-04-17 (Friday)**
- Morning: Take the train to Akihabara — Tokyo's legendary Electric Town and the epicenter of tech and otaku culture. Spend the morning exploring multi-floor electronics shops like Yodobashi Akiba, retro game stores, and component shops selling cutting-edge gadgets and rare tech finds.
- Afternoon: Dive deeper into Akihabara's subculture: visit a maid café for the quintessential experience (¥600–¥1,000 for a drink + performance), browse anime merchandise floors, and explore the Akihabara Radio Kaikan building — a tech-lover's paradise with floors of electronics, figures, and hobby shops.
- Evening: Head to nearby Kanda/Ochanomizu for dinner in a more local neighborhood, then return to the hotel via a pleasant evening walk through Nihonbashi — Tokyo's historic commercial heart with beautiful Meiji-era architecture lit up at night.
- Notes: Akihabara is best explored on foot — the main street and side alleys both have distinct characters. Free Wi-Fi is available throughout. Some maid cafés require reservations; walk-ins usually available on weekday afternoons.

**2027-04-18 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Visit teamLab Planets in Toyosu (book tickets in advance online, ¥3,200). This world-famous digital art immersive experience combines technology, nature, and culture in breathtaking installations. Go early to beat weekend crowds.
- Afternoon: Explore Toyosu Market — the modern successor to Tsukiji's wholesale operations. Take the official market tour to see the famous tuna auctions (viewing area, pre-registration required) or simply explore the market building's restaurants and shops. Then walk along the Toyosu waterfront for views of Rainbow Bridge and the bay.
- Evening: Head to Odaiba — Tokyo's futuristic artificial island. See the life-size Unicorn Gundam statue (free), browse DiverCity Tokyo Plaza, and enjoy the spectacular nighttime view of Rainbow Bridge and the Tokyo skyline from the waterfront promenade.
- Notes: teamLab Planets tickets must be booked online in advance — they frequently sell out, especially on weekends. Wear socks as shoes are removed inside. Odaiba is accessible via the scenic Yurikamome driverless monorail — worth the ride for the views.

**2027-04-19 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Take the train to Harajuku for the Sunday Takeshita Street experience — colorful fashion, crepe shops, and Tokyo's most expressive youth culture. Then walk through the serene Yoyogi Park, especially beautiful during cherry blossom season with locals picnicking under the trees.
- Afternoon: Visit Meiji Jingu Shrine — a peaceful forested sanctuary in the heart of the city. Walk the gravel path through towering torii gates, observe traditional Shinto rituals, and browse the votive plaques. Then explore the upscale Omotesando boulevard — Tokyo's Champs-Élysées — with its striking architectural flagship stores.
- Evening: Explore Omotesando Hills and the backstreets of Aoyama for boutique shopping and gallery browsing. The area transitions beautifully into evening with well-lit cafés and restaurants.
- Notes: Meiji Jingu is free to enter. Yoyogi Park is especially lively on Sundays. Omotesando is excellent for window shopping even on a mid-range budget — the architecture alone is worth seeing. April cherry blossoms in Yoyogi Park may still be blooming.

**2027-04-20 (Monday)**
- Morning: Head to Shinjuku for a full day of exploration. Start with the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation deck (free!) for panoramic city views — arrive early for the clearest skies and shortest wait.
- Afternoon: Explore Shinjuku's contrasting neighborhoods: the organized chaos of Kabukicho entertainment district by day, the peaceful Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (¥500 entry, stunning in cherry blossom season), and the narrow alleyways of Golden Gai and Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) — a cluster of tiny postwar bars and eateries.
- Evening: Experience Shinjuku at night — one of Tokyo's most electric experiences. Walk through the neon-lit streets of East Shinjuku, watch the famous Shinjuku scramble crossing, and explore the department store basement food halls (depachika) at Takashimaya or Isetan for incredible prepared foods.
- Notes: The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building has two observation towers — the North Tower is usually open until 10:30 PM and is free. Shinjuku Gyoen is closed on Mondays — substitute with Shinjuku Central Park if so. Golden Gai bars are tiny (some seat only 6 people) and very welcoming to respectful tourists.

**2027-04-21 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Nikko — a UNESCO World Heritage site about 2 hours from Tokyo by Tobu Nikko Line (¥1,360 one way or consider the Tobu Nikko Pass). Visit the lavishly decorated Toshogu Shrine complex, built in 1617 to enshrine Tokugawa Ieyasu, featuring intricate carvings and the famous three wise monkeys.
- Afternoon: Continue exploring Nikko's sacred sites: Rinno-ji Temple with its giant golden Buddha statues, Futarasan Jinja Shrine, and the beautiful Shinkyo Bridge (Sacred Bridge) over the Daiya River. If time permits, walk up to Taiyuin Mausoleum — quieter and equally stunning.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo by early evening. Arrive back in Tsukiji/Ginza area for a relaxed dinner close to the hotel.
- Notes: Depart early (by 8:00–8:30 AM) to maximize time in Nikko. The Tobu Nikko All Area Pass (¥4,780) covers round-trip train and buses within Nikko — excellent value. Nikko can be cold and misty in April — bring a light jacket. Toshogu entry is ¥1,300.

**2027-04-22 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Explore the Ginza district — Tokyo's most prestigious shopping and cultural neighborhood, just a short walk from your Tsukiji hotel. Visit the Itoya stationery flagship store (9 floors of beautiful Japanese paper goods and design), browse the free gallery spaces in Ginza's art buildings, and admire the architecture of flagship stores by world-renowned architects.
- Afternoon: Visit the Mori Art Museum or the National Art Center in nearby Roppongi (check current exhibitions — typically ¥1,800–¥2,000). Roppongi Hills also features the free Mori Tower observation sky deck lobby and excellent public art installations including Louise Bourgeois's iconic Maman spider sculpture.
- Evening: Stay in Roppongi for the evening — the area transforms at night. Visit Tokyo Midtown's beautifully lit gardens, then enjoy dinner in the neighborhood before heading back to Tsukiji.
- Notes: Many Ginza galleries and art spaces are free. The Itoya stationery store is a must for design and culture enthusiasts. Roppongi Hills and Tokyo Midtown are free to explore — only specific museum exhibitions require tickets. The walk from Tsukiji to Ginza takes about 15–20 minutes.

**2027-04-23 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit the teamLab Borderless digital art museum in Azabudai Hills (new 2024 location — book tickets online, ¥3,800). A more expansive and exploratory experience than Planets, this is one of the world's most innovative intersections of technology, art, and culture. Allow 2–3 hours minimum.
- Afternoon: Explore the new Azabudai Hills complex itself — a cutting-edge mixed-use development that represents Tokyo's vision of future urban living. Then head to nearby Minato ward to explore the Zojo-ji Temple with Tokyo Tower looming dramatically behind it — a perfect cultural contrast between ancient and modern.
- Evening: Climb Tokyo Tower (¥1,200 for main deck) at dusk for spectacular views as the city lights up. The tower is smaller and more intimate than Skytree, with a nostalgic Showa-era charm. Return to Tsukiji for dinner.
- Notes: teamLab Borderless tickets must be booked well in advance online. Zojo-ji Temple is free to enter and the garden behind the temple has hundreds of Jizo statues — a moving and photogenic sight. Tokyo Tower's observation deck is best visited at dusk for both daytime and nighttime views.

**2027-04-24 (Friday)**
- Morning: Explore Yanaka — one of Tokyo's best-preserved old shitamachi (downtown) neighborhoods that survived WWII bombing. Walk through Yanaka Cemetery (beautiful in cherry blossom season), browse the Yanaka Ginza shopping street with traditional craft shops and local food stalls, and feel the atmosphere of old Tokyo.
- Afternoon: Continue to the Ueno museum district. Visit the Tokyo National Museum — Japan's largest museum with an extraordinary collection of Japanese art, samurai armor, ceramics, and ancient artifacts (¥1,000). The museum grounds also include a beautiful Japanese garden open seasonally.
- Evening: Explore Ueno's Ameyoko Market — a lively open-air market street with food stalls, fresh produce, discount goods, and street food. The evening atmosphere is especially vibrant on Fridays. Then enjoy dinner in the area before returning to Tsukiji.
- Notes: Yanaka is best explored slowly on foot — allow at least 2 hours. The Tokyo National Museum is closed on Mondays. Ameyoko is busiest and most atmospheric on weekday evenings and weekends. The Yanaka–Nezu–Yanesen area is excellent for authentic Tokyo photography.

**2027-04-25 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Kamakura — about 1 hour from Tokyo by JR Yokosuka Line. Start with the iconic Kotoku-in Great Buddha (Daibutsu) — a 13-meter bronze Buddha dating from 1252. Then walk the coastal Daibutsu Hiking Trail through bamboo groves and forested hills.
- Afternoon: Explore Kamakura's temple circuit: Hase-dera Temple with its stunning hillside garden and sea views, Zeniarai Benzaiten Shrine (wash your money in the cave spring for good fortune), and the charming Komachi-dori shopping street for local crafts, matcha sweets, and souvenirs.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo in the early evening. Stop at Yokohama's Chinatown (en route) if time permits for a quick exploration of Japan's largest Chinatown. Back in Tsukiji, enjoy a final evening walk along the Sumida River.
- Notes: Depart by 8:00–8:30 AM. JR Pass holders can use it for the Yokosuka Line. Kamakura's Great Buddha entry is ¥300. Hase-dera entry is ¥400. April weather in Kamakura is mild and pleasant — the temple gardens may have late cherry blossoms or wisteria beginning to bloom. Wear comfortable walking shoes for the hiking trail.

**2027-04-26 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit Tokyo Skytree — Japan's tallest structure at 634 meters. Book tickets online in advance (¥2,100 for Tembo Deck at 350m, or ¥3,400 including the Tembo Galleria at 450m). The views on a clear morning are extraordinary, extending to Mt. Fuji on good days.
- Afternoon: Explore the Skytree's base area — Solamachi shopping complex has excellent food, traditional craft shops, and a planetarium. Then cross to nearby Kappabashi Dori — Tokyo's wholesale kitchenware street, a fascinating cultural experience where you can buy professional Japanese knives, lacquerware, ceramics, and the famous plastic food models.
- Evening: Final evening in Tokyo — return to Asakusa for a nostalgic last stroll through Senso-ji at night. The illuminated temple and pagoda are magical. Celebrate your final night with a special dinner experience.
- Notes: Tokyo Skytree tickets sell out on weekends — book online at least a few days ahead. Kappabashi Dori is a must for food culture enthusiasts — the Japanese knife shops in particular are world-class. Pack any purchases carefully. Begin thinking about what to pack and what to leave — luggage forwarding services (takkyubin) can send bags to the airport from convenience stores.

**2027-04-27 (Monday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Tsukiji — one last breakfast at the Outer Market, saying goodbye to the vendors and flavors that bookended your trip. Do any last-minute souvenir shopping: the market area has excellent Japanese kitchen tools, teas, and food gifts.
- Afternoon: Check out of hotel (standard checkout 11:00 AM — confirm with hotel). Store luggage if your flight is later. Take a final walk through Ginza or a nearby neighborhood. Visit a depachika (department store basement food hall) at Mitsukoshi or Matsuya in Ginza for beautifully packaged Japanese food souvenirs and sweets to bring home.
- Evening: Head to the airport. Allow 2–2.5 hours before departure. Narita: take Narita Express (N'EX) from Shinjuku or Tokyo Station (~¥3,070, ~60 min). Haneda: take Tokyo Monorail from Hamamatsucho or Keikyu Line from Shinagawa (~¥700, ~30 min).
- Notes: Redeem any remaining Suica/Pasmo balance at the airport or keep the card for a future visit (cards are valid for 10 years). Declare any food items when returning home — many Japanese food products are permitted but rules vary by country. Keep receipts for tax refund claims (tax-free shopping requires passport at purchase). Sayonara, Tokyo!
