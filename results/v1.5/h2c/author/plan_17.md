## Day by day

**2027-03-01**
- Morning: Arrival at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Transfer from airport to boutique hotel in Centro Histórico (approximately 45–60 min by taxi or Uber, ~$15–20 USD). Check in, freshen up, and get oriented.
- Afternoon: Take a leisurely walk around the Zócalo (Plaza de la Constitución), one of the largest public squares in the world. Explore the exterior of the Metropolitan Cathedral and admire the Palacio Nacional facade. Stroll down Calle Madero, a vibrant pedestrian street lined with historic architecture, street performers, and local vendors.
- Evening: Head to the Templo Mayor archaeological site area for a golden-hour atmosphere, then enjoy your first proper Mexico City dinner nearby. Explore the side streets of Centro Histórico as the city lights up at night.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light given the afternoon arrival. Comfortable walking shoes are essential. Exchange some currency or use ATMs in the hotel area for convenience.

**2027-03-02**
- Morning: Visit Templo Mayor Museum (open from 9:00 AM) — an extraordinary archaeological site right in the heart of Centro Histórico, built on the ruins of the Aztec capital Tenochtitlán. Allow 1.5–2 hours. Entry ~$5 USD.
- Afternoon: Walk to the Palacio de Bellas Artes to admire its stunning Art Nouveau and Art Deco architecture. Visit the murals inside by Diego Rivera and other Mexican masters (entry to ground floor is free; upper galleries ~$3 USD). Afterward, explore the nearby Alameda Central park for a relaxing stroll among locals.
- Evening: Explore the bohemian neighborhood of Barrio Chino (Chinatown) or head to the lively streets around República de Uruguay for street food exploration. End the evening with a mezcal tasting at a local mezcalería.
- Notes: Templo Mayor is closed on Mondays — if Day 2 falls on a Monday, swap with the Palacio de Bellas Artes morning visit. Carry a light jacket as March mornings can be cool.

**2027-03-03**
- Morning: Day trip to Teotihuacán — take an early bus from Terminal Norte (about 1 hour, ~$3 USD each way) to explore the ancient pyramids of the Sun and Moon. Climb the Pyramid of the Sun for panoramic views and walk the Avenue of the Dead. Arrive early (site opens at 9:00 AM) to beat crowds and heat. Entry ~$5 USD.
- Afternoon: Return to Mexico City by early afternoon. Stop by the neighborhood of Tlatelolco on the way back to see the Plaza de las Tres Culturas — a powerful site where Aztec ruins, a colonial church, and modern architecture coexist.
- Evening: Relax in Centro Histórico and explore the street food scene around Plaza Garibaldi, where mariachi bands perform nightly. Try street snacks like elotes (corn) and churros.
- Notes: Wear sunscreen and bring water for Teotihuacán — the site is very exposed. Start as early as possible (ideally by 8:30 AM at the bus terminal). Comfortable, sturdy shoes are a must.

**2027-03-04**
- Morning: Take the Metro (Line 3, ~$0.30 USD) south to the Coyoacán neighborhood. Visit the Frida Kahlo Museum (Casa Azul) — book tickets in advance online as they sell out quickly (~$12 USD). Explore the iconic blue house and her personal belongings, art, and life story.
- Afternoon: Wander through Coyoacán's charming streets and central market (Mercado de Coyoacán) — a colorful local market perfect for trying antojitos and picking up handmade souvenirs. Visit the nearby Diego Rivera Anahuacalli Museum if time allows (~$5 USD).
- Evening: Head back toward Centro or explore the nearby Colonia Roma neighborhood (a short Uber ride) for its trendy café and restaurant scene, tree-lined streets, and Art Deco architecture.
- Notes: Book Frida Kahlo Museum tickets at least 1–2 weeks in advance at museofridakahlo.org.mx. Coyoacán is best explored on foot — it's a very walkable, safe neighborhood popular with families and artists.

**2027-03-05**
- Morning: Visit the Museo Nacional de Antropología (National Museum of Anthropology) in Chapultepec — one of the greatest museums in the world, housing the Aztec Sun Stone and priceless pre-Hispanic artifacts. Allow at least 2–3 hours. Entry ~$5 USD. Located inside Bosque de Chapultepec, Latin America's largest urban park.
- Afternoon: Stroll through Chapultepec Park, visit the Chapultepec Castle (Castillo de Chapultepec) for sweeping city views and Mexican history exhibits (~$4 USD entry). Afterward, explore the upscale Polanco neighborhood nearby — window shop on Presidente Masaryk Avenue and enjoy the cosmopolitan atmosphere.
- Evening: Return to Centro Histórico for a final evening. Pick up last-minute souvenirs at artisan shops on Calle Moneda or near the Zócalo. Enjoy a farewell dinner with a view.
- Notes: The Anthropology Museum is closed on Mondays — plan accordingly. Chapultepec Park is very popular on weekends with families. Keep valuables secure in crowded areas. Check flight departure time for Day 6 to plan final morning accordingly.
