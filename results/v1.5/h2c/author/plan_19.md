## Day by day

**2027-11-15**
- Morning: Arrival at Siem Reap International Airport at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Transfer to Siem Reap Boutique Stay and check in.
- Afternoon: After settling in, take a leisurely stroll through the Old Market (Psar Chas) area to get oriented. Browse local handicrafts, silk scarves, and Khmer souvenirs. Visit the nearby Preah Ang Chek Preah Ang Chorm Shrine for a gentle cultural introduction.
- Evening: Explore the famous Pub Street and Alley West for atmosphere, then enjoy a relaxed first evening in the city.
- Notes: Exchange currency to Cambodian Riel (KHR) or use USD at the airport or a local bank. Tuk-tuks from the airport to the hotel cost approximately $7–10. November is the beginning of the dry season — expect pleasant, cooler weather with low humidity.

**2027-11-16**
- Morning: Early morning visit to Angkor Wat (arrive by 5:30 AM for sunrise). Watch the iconic temple reflect in the lotus pond as the sun rises over its towers — one of the most breathtaking sights in Southeast Asia. Spend the morning exploring the outer galleries, bas-reliefs depicting the Churning of the Ocean of Milk, and the central sanctuary.
- Afternoon: Continue within the Angkor Archaeological Park to Angkor Thom — visit the Bayon Temple with its 216 serene stone faces, the Baphuon, Phimeanakas, and the Terrace of the Elephants. Take a break at a nearby shaded food stall.
- Evening: Return to Siem Reap and enjoy a traditional Apsara dance performance over dinner at Koulen II Restaurant or Angkor Village Theatre.
- Notes: Purchase a 3-day Angkor Pass (~$62 USD) — it offers the best value for multiple temple visits. Hire a knowledgeable tuk-tuk driver for the day (~$15–20) who can navigate efficiently. Bring sunscreen, a hat, and at least 1.5 liters of water. Modest dress required — shoulders and knees must be covered.

**2027-11-17**
- Morning: Use your Angkor Pass for a second day of temple exploration. Head to Ta Prohm — the jungle temple made famous by the film Tomb Raider — where massive silk-cotton tree roots engulf ancient stone walls. Follow with Banteay Kdei and the beautiful Srah Srang reservoir.
- Afternoon: Visit the remote and exquisite Banteay Srei Temple (~35 km northeast of Siem Reap), renowned for its intricate pink sandstone carvings considered the finest in all of Angkor. Then stop at the Landmine Museum nearby to learn about Cambodia's tragic recent history and de-mining efforts.
- Evening: Back in Siem Reap, visit the vibrant Night Market along the river for local crafts, handmade goods, and street food.
- Notes: Banteay Srei is best visited in the afternoon when crowds thin slightly. The Landmine Museum (RCAF) charges a small entry fee (~$3) and is deeply moving — highly recommended for historical context. Tuk-tuk for the full day including Banteay Srei trip: ~$20–25.

**2027-11-18**
- Morning: Third and final day on the Angkor Pass. Explore the lesser-visited temples of the Roluos Group — Preah Ko, Bakong, and Lolei — some of the oldest temples in the Angkor region dating to the 9th century. These are quieter and offer a more intimate experience.
- Afternoon: Visit the Angkor National Museum in Siem Reap for deep historical context on the Khmer Empire, its art, and its civilization. Excellent multimedia exhibits and a stunning collection of Buddha statues.
- Evening: Attend a cooking class at a local culinary school (e.g., Cooks in Tuk Tuks or Le Tigre de Papier) — learn to make Amok, green papaya salad, and banana blossom dishes.
- Notes: The Roluos Group is about 13 km east of Siem Reap — factor in 30-minute travel time. Book the cooking class in advance online. November evenings can be slightly cool — bring a light layer for the cooking class.

**2027-11-19**
- Morning: Take a half-day excursion to Tonlé Sap Lake — one of the largest freshwater lakes in Southeast Asia and a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Board a boat to visit a floating village (Kampong Phluk or Chong Kneas) and observe the unique stilted houses, floating schools, and fishing communities.
- Afternoon: Return to Siem Reap and visit the Artisans Angkor workshop — a social enterprise training young Cambodians in traditional silk weaving, lacquerware, and stone carving. Free guided tours available and a great place to purchase authentic, ethically made souvenirs.
- Evening: Relax with a traditional Khmer massage at one of Siem Reap's reputable spas (e.g., Seeing Hands Massage, which employs visually impaired therapists) followed by a quiet dinner.
- Notes: Boat tours to Tonlé Sap typically cost $15–25 per person. Go with a reputable operator to ensure the community benefits from tourism. Sunset on the lake is stunning in November. Seeing Hands Massage: ~$8–12 per hour — book ahead as it's popular.

**2027-11-20**
- Morning: Depart Siem Reap by bus or private taxi to Phnom Penh (approximately 5–6 hours by road or 45 minutes by domestic flight). If traveling by bus, Giant Ibis is the most comfortable and reliable option (~$15 per person).
- Afternoon: Arrive in Phnom Penh. Check in at a mid-range hotel (e.g., Plantation Hotel or Pavilion Hotel). Visit the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda — a stunning complex of Khmer royal architecture with a floor made of 5,000 silver tiles and a life-size gold Buddha encrusted with diamonds.
- Evening: Stroll along the Sisowath Quay (Riverside Promenade) as the sun sets over the Tonlé Sap and Mekong rivers. Browse the lively riverside atmosphere.
- Notes: Book Giant Ibis bus tickets in advance online. The Royal Palace closes at noon for a lunch break (12:00–14:00) — time your arrival accordingly. Entry fee to Royal Palace: ~$10. Phnom Penh is notably hotter and more urban than Siem Reap.

**2027-11-21**
- Morning: Visit the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (S-21 Prison) — a former high school turned Khmer Rouge detention and torture center. This sobering and essential site provides profound insight into Cambodia's tragic recent history. Allow 2–3 hours.
- Afternoon: Travel 15 km south of Phnom Penh to the Choeung Ek Killing Fields Memorial — a deeply moving site with an audio guide narrating personal survivor stories. Afterward, visit the Central Market (Phsar Thmei) — a striking Art Deco building with stalls selling jewelry, clothing, and local goods.
- Evening: Visit the National Museum of Cambodia, home to the world's finest collection of Khmer sculpture and artifacts. End the evening with a sunset cruise on the Mekong River.
- Notes: The Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek visits are emotionally heavy — plan a quiet, reflective afternoon. Entry: Tuol Sleng ~$5, Choeung Ek ~$6 (includes audio guide). Mekong sunset cruise: ~$10–15 per person. National Museum entry: ~$10.

**2027-11-22**
- Morning: Depart Phnom Penh by bus or shared taxi to Sihanoukville (approximately 4 hours), then take a ferry to Koh Rong Samloem Island — one of Cambodia's most pristine and beautiful islands with turquoise waters and white sand beaches.
- Afternoon: Arrive at Koh Rong Samloem, check in at a mid-range beachfront bungalow (e.g., Lazy Beach Resort or Saracen Bay Resort). Spend the afternoon relaxing on Saracen Bay beach, swimming in crystal-clear water, and unwinding.
- Evening: Watch the sunset from the beach with a cocktail in hand. After dark, experience the magical bioluminescent plankton that glows blue in the water when disturbed — a truly unforgettable natural phenomenon.
- Notes: Book ferry tickets in advance through Speed Ferry Cambodia or GTVC (~$25–35 round trip). Ferries from Sihanoukville to Koh Rong Samloem run several times daily. The island has limited ATMs — bring sufficient cash. Bioluminescent plankton is best viewed during new moon phases; November conditions are generally favorable.

**2027-11-23**
- Morning: Enjoy a leisurely morning on the beach. Rent a kayak or paddleboard (~$5–10/hour) and explore the coastline. Snorkel around the coral reefs just offshore — the water clarity in November is excellent.
- Afternoon: Hike through the island's jungle interior to the secluded M'Pai Bay village on the north side of the island — a 45-minute scenic trail offering views of both coastlines. Explore the small fishing community and its quieter beach.
- Evening: Return to Saracen Bay for a final beach sunset. Enjoy a bonfire dinner on the beach if your resort offers it, or dine under the stars at a beachfront restaurant.
- Notes: Wear sturdy sandals or light hiking shoes for the jungle trail to M'Pai Bay. Bring insect repellent. Snorkeling gear can be rented from most beach bungalows (~$5/day). The island has no cars — everything is walkable or by boat.

**2027-11-24**
- Morning: Final morning on Koh Rong Samloem — take one last swim, collect shells, and soak in the tranquility. Pack up and take the morning ferry back to Sihanoukville, then connect to Phnom Penh or Siem Reap for your departure flight.
- Afternoon: Depending on your departure flight, use any remaining time in Phnom Penh or Siem Reap to pick up last-minute souvenirs — silk products, Kampot pepper, Cambodian coffee, or handcrafted goods from Artisans Angkor.
- Evening: Departure preparation — final Cambodian meal, airport transfer, and farewell to Cambodia.
- Notes: Morning ferries from Koh Rong Samloem depart around 7:00–9:00 AM — confirm the schedule the night before. Allow at least 3–4 hours of buffer time between the ferry and any domestic/international flight. Domestic flights from Sihanoukville to Phnom Penh or Siem Reap are available (~$50–80). Ensure your passport and travel documents are accessible. Cambodia departure tax is typically included in your ticket.
