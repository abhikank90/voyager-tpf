## Day by day

**2027-08-15 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Arrival at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Flight LH lands at 1:00 PM. Collect luggage, pick up rental car, and drive to Hanauma Bay, East Oahu Boutique Stay (~30–45 min from HNL).
- Afternoon: Check in to the boutique stay, unpack, and take a leisurely stroll along the shoreline near Hanauma Bay to get your bearings. Enjoy the ocean views and let the family decompress from travel.
- Evening: Head to nearby Hawaii Kai for a relaxed welcome dinner. Explore the Hawaii Kai Towne Center area and settle into island time.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light — travel fatigue is real, especially with kids. Pre-book your rental car to avoid long waits. Stock up on sunscreen, snacks, and water at a nearby Safeway or Times Supermarket before heading to the hotel.

**2027-08-16 (Monday)**
- Morning: Snorkeling at Hanauma Bay — Reserve your entry tickets online in advance (required). Arrive early (8:00–8:30 AM) before crowds build. Watch the mandatory marine education video, then spend 2–3 hours exploring the stunning coral reef teeming with tropical fish. Perfect for all family ages.
- Afternoon: After Hanauma Bay, drive 10 minutes to Sandy Beach Park for some bodyboarding or simply relaxing on the sand. Watch the local bodyboarders tackle the shore break — it's a spectator sport in itself. Then visit nearby Makapu'u Lookout for panoramic ocean views.
- Evening: Return to the hotel to rinse off and rest, then head out for a casual dinner back in Hawaii Kai.
- Notes: Hanauma Bay has a $25/person entry fee for non-residents (children under 12 are free). Tickets sell out fast — book online at least 1–2 days ahead. Bring reef-safe sunscreen only (required by Hawaii law). Snorkel gear can be rented on-site.

**2027-08-17 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Drive to Waikiki (~25 min from Hanauma Bay) for Surfing Lessons at Waikiki Beach. Book a family surf lesson with Hans Hedemannn Surf School or Aloha Beach Services — certified instructors teach beginners on the gentle, forgiving waves of Waikiki. Lessons typically run 1.5–2 hours and are suitable for all ages.
- Afternoon: After lessons, spend free time on Waikiki Beach — build sandcastles, swim in the calm water, or rent a paddleboard. Walk along Kalakaua Avenue and browse the shops. Visit the Duke Kahanamoku Statue for a photo op.
- Evening: Stay in Waikiki for dinner, then catch the free Royal Hawaiian Center hula show (check schedule in advance) on the outdoor stage.
- Notes: Surfing lesson prices typically range from $50–$75/person for group lessons. Book at least a day in advance. Parking in Waikiki can be expensive — use the Waikiki Trade Center garage or consider the Bus from Hawaii Kai. Apply waterproof sunscreen before lessons.

**2027-08-18 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Drive to the North Shore of Oahu (~1 hour from Hanauma Bay). Stop at the Dole Pineapple Plantation for a fun family activity — take the Pineapple Express Train Tour and explore the world's largest outdoor maze. Kids love it and it's a great introduction to Hawaii's agricultural heritage.
- Afternoon: Continue to Sunset Beach and Waimea Bay on the North Shore. Walk along the beach, explore Waimea Valley (botanical garden with a freshwater swimming hole and waterfall — great for families), and soak in the legendary North Shore atmosphere.
- Evening: On the drive back, stop through Haleiwa Town for shave ice at Matsumoto's — a Hawaii bucket-list experience. Then return to the hotel.
- Notes: Waimea Valley charges ~$20/adult and $10/child for entry. The waterfall swim is seasonal — check conditions. North Shore waves in August are typically calm (big wave season is winter), making it safe for swimming. The round trip drive is about 2 hours total — plan for a full day.

**2027-08-19 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Polynesian Cultural Center Visit — Drive ~45 minutes to Laie on the windward coast. Arrive when it opens to maximize your time. Spend the morning exploring the six Polynesian island villages (Hawaii, Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, Tahiti, and Aotearoa), watching cultural demonstrations, and participating in hands-on activities like coconut husking, poi pounding, and fire-making.
- Afternoon: Continue exploring the PCC villages and catch the afternoon canoe pageant on the lagoon — a colorful, music-filled parade of traditional canoes representing each island culture. Allow time for the IMAX film or the Hā: Breath of Life evening show (book in advance).
- Evening: Attend the Ali'i Luau at the Polynesian Cultural Center — a traditional Hawaiian luau with an imu ceremony (underground oven), all-you-can-eat Hawaiian buffet, and live entertainment. This is a highlight experience for families.
- Notes: Book PCC tickets well in advance — the Ali'i Luau sells out quickly in summer. Full-day packages including the luau range from $120–$160/person (children discounted). The PCC is run by Brigham Young University Hawaii — it's alcohol-free. Plan to spend 6–8 hours here. Wear comfortable walking shoes.

**2027-08-20 (Friday)**
- Morning: Rest and recovery morning — enjoy a slow start at the hotel. Take a gentle morning swim or snorkel right at Hanauma Bay area (if energy allows) or simply relax on the lanai. This mid-trip rest day is important, especially for families with young children.
- Afternoon: Drive to Kailua Beach (~20 min from Hanauma Bay) — consistently ranked one of the best beaches in America. The calm, turquoise water and powdery white sand are perfect for swimming, kayaking, and building sandcastles. Rent kayaks or stand-up paddleboards from Kailua Beach Adventures to paddle out to the Mokulua Islands (the 'Mokes').
- Evening: Explore charming Kailua Town for dinner and an evening stroll. Browse the local boutiques and galleries before heading back to the hotel.
- Notes: Kailua Beach parking can fill up on weekends — arrive before 10 AM or use the town parking lots. Kayak rentals typically cost $30–$50 for a half-day. The paddle to the Moku Nui island (part of the Mokulua Islands) takes about 30–45 minutes each way and is suitable for families with older children.

**2027-08-21 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Inter-island flight to Maui — Book an early morning flight from Honolulu (HNL) to Kahului (OGG) on Hawaiian Airlines or Southwest (~30 min flight). Pick up a rental car at OGG airport and drive toward the Road to Hana.
- Afternoon: Road to Hana Drive — Begin the legendary scenic drive along Maui's northeastern coastline. Stop at Twin Falls (easy family hike to waterfalls), the Garden of Eden Arboretum (optional paid stop with beautiful botanical gardens), and Wai'anapanapa State Park (stunning black sand beach — reserve entry online in advance).
- Evening: Arrive in Hana Town for the night. Explore the small, charming town and enjoy a quiet evening dinner. Stay at a simple inn or guesthouse in Hana if doing the full Road to Hana experience, or begin the return drive if doing it as a day trip.
- Notes: The Road to Hana is ~52 miles one way with 600+ curves and 59 bridges — allow 3–4 hours each way without stops. Start early to avoid traffic. Wai'anapanapa State Park requires advance reservations ($5/person entry). Gas up in Paia before the drive — there are no gas stations along the route until Hana. This is a long day — pack snacks, water, and motion sickness remedies for kids.

**2027-08-22 (Sunday)**
- Morning: If staying overnight in Hana, continue the Road to Hana beyond Hana Town to Ohe'o Gulch (Seven Sacred Pools) at Haleakala National Park — a series of gorgeous tiered pools and waterfalls. Swim in the lower pools (when open) and hike the short Kuloa Point Trail for panoramic views.
- Afternoon: Begin the return drive back to Kahului, stopping at Maui Wine (Ulupalakua Ranch) for a scenic detour through upcountry Maui. Visit the charming town of Makawao for boutique shopping and local art galleries. Drive down to Kihei or Wailea on Maui's sunny south shore for a late afternoon beach swim.
- Evening: Catch your inter-island return flight from OGG back to HNL (evening flight), return rental car, and transfer back to the Hanauma Bay boutique stay.
- Notes: Ohe'o Gulch swimming access depends on current conditions and park regulations — check the National Park Service website before visiting. The return drive via upcountry Maui (through Kula and Makawao) is less congested than retracing the northern route. Book inter-island flights at least a week ahead for best prices (~$60–$120/person round trip on Hawaiian or Southwest).

**2027-08-23 (Monday)**
- Morning: Morning at leisure near the hotel — revisit Hanauma Bay for a final snorkel session (no reservation needed if you have an existing pass, or book ahead) or explore the nearby Koko Head Crater Trail for a challenging but rewarding hike with panoramic views of East Oahu. The trail involves climbing 1,048 old railway steps — great for active families with older kids.
- Afternoon: Drive to Diamond Head State Monument (~20 min from Hanauma Bay) for the iconic Diamond Head Crater hike. The 1.6-mile round-trip trail leads to the summit of the famous volcanic tuff cone with sweeping views of Waikiki and the Pacific. Book entry tickets online in advance. Then spend the remaining afternoon at Ala Moana Beach Park — a long, beautiful beach adjacent to the shopping center.
- Evening: Visit Ala Moana Center — Hawaii's largest open-air shopping mall — for souvenir shopping, people-watching, and dinner. Catch the free hula show at the Ala Moana Center stage (check schedule).
- Notes: Diamond Head requires advance online reservations ($5/person entry fee; parking is $10). The hike takes about 1–1.5 hours round trip. Bring water and wear sturdy shoes. Koko Head Trail is very strenuous — not recommended for young children or those with mobility issues. The Ala Moana hula show schedule varies — check the Ala Moana Center website.

**2027-08-24 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Hawaii — wake up early for one last sunrise at Hanauma Bay overlook or Makapu'u Lighthouse Trail (a paved, accessible 2-mile round-trip trail with stunning coastal views and seasonal whale sightings). Pack up the hotel room and check out.
- Afternoon: Drive to Honolulu for final souvenir shopping at the International Market Place in Waikiki or the Aloha Stadium Swap Meet (if available). Visit the Bishop Museum (Hawaii's premier natural and cultural history museum) if time allows — excellent for families with curious kids.
- Evening: Head to the airport for departure. Allow at least 2.5–3 hours before your flight for check-in, security, and the long walk to gates at HNL. Grab a final malasada from Leonard's Bakery en route to the airport as a sweet farewell.
- Notes: Check out time is typically 11 AM — arrange late checkout or luggage storage with the hotel if your flight departs in the evening. Return the rental car with a full tank of gas to avoid refueling fees. HNL airport has excellent food options post-security. Don't forget to declare any fresh fruits, flowers, or agricultural products — they may not be allowed on mainland flights.
