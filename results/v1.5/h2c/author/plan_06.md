## Day by day

**2027-04-15 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Arrival at airport at 13:00 — clear customs, collect luggage, and take the Narita Express or Limousine Bus to Tsukiji area. Check in to your boutique stay and freshen up.
- Afternoon: Take a leisurely stroll through the Tsukiji Outer Market (築地場外市場) to get your bearings. Browse fresh seafood snacks, tamagoyaki egg stalls, and local vendors. Pick up a light snack to tide you over — try grilled scallops or fresh tuna nigiri from a market stall.
- Evening: Walk to nearby Hamarikyu Gardens (浜離宮恩賜庭園) for a peaceful evening stroll along the waterfront and tidal pond — a beautiful contrast to the urban skyline. Then head back for dinner in the Tsukiji neighborhood.
- Notes: Jet lag may kick in — keep Day 1 light and relaxed. Avoid heavy activities. Purchase a Suica or Pasmo IC card at the airport for seamless transit throughout the trip. April in Tokyo is cherry blossom season — expect beautiful scenery.

**2027-04-16 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Wake up early and visit Tsukiji Outer Market at its freshest (7:00–9:00 AM). Enjoy a proper breakfast at one of the market's beloved breakfast spots — fresh sashimi bowls or a classic Japanese breakfast set. Then walk to Ginza (銀座), Tokyo's upscale shopping and cultural district, just 10 minutes away.
- Afternoon: Explore Ginza's galleries and architecture. Visit the Itoya flagship stationery store (a must for design lovers) and the Sony Park or Ginza Six atrium for a taste of Tokyo's tech-meets-culture retail scene. Browse the free gallery floors of department stores like Matsuya Ginza.
- Evening: Head to Shiodome (汐留) — the futuristic media and corporate district adjacent to Ginza — for an evening walk among illuminated skyscrapers. Visit the Caretta Shiodome mall and enjoy the free illumination displays.
- Notes: Ginza galleries are often free. Depachika (department store basement food halls) are a Tokyo cultural experience in themselves — don't miss them. Shimbashi is a great spot to observe Tokyo's salaryman culture at izakayas in the evening.

**2027-04-17 (Friday)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Asakusa (浅草) — Tokyo's most iconic traditional district. Visit Senso-ji Temple (浅草寺) early morning before crowds build. Walk Nakamise Shopping Street for traditional crafts, snacks, and souvenirs. Explore the backstreets of Yanaka-style old Tokyo atmosphere.
- Afternoon: Visit the nearby Tokyo Skytree (東京スカイツリー) — the world's tallest broadcast tower. Purchase tickets for the Tembo Deck (350m) for panoramic views of Tokyo. Explore the Solamachi shopping complex at the base for tech gadgets, local food products, and Japanese design goods.
- Evening: Explore the Asakusa entertainment and dining district. Walk along the Sumida River promenade, especially beautiful during cherry blossom season. Enjoy a traditional tempura or eel (unagi) dinner in Asakusa.
- Notes: Skytree tickets: ~¥2,100 for Tembo Deck. Book online in advance to skip queues. Senso-ji is free to enter. Friday evenings in Asakusa are lively — great for people-watching.

**2027-04-18 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Head to Akihabara (秋葉原) — Tokyo's legendary Electric Town and global epicenter of tech culture. Explore multi-floor electronics shops like Yodobashi Camera and Bic Camera. Browse retro game shops, anime merchandise stores, and cutting-edge gadget boutiques.
- Afternoon: Continue exploring Akihabara's tech culture — visit the Radio Kaikan building for vintage electronics and collectibles. Stop by a maid café for a uniquely Tokyo cultural experience. Then walk or take a short train to Ochanomizu and Jimbocho (神保町) — Tokyo's famous book and vintage culture district.
- Evening: Return to the Tsukiji/Ginza area or stay in Akihabara for dinner. Explore the vibrant Saturday evening atmosphere.
- Notes: Akihabara is extremely busy on Saturdays. Some streets are pedestrianized on weekend afternoons. Maid café experience costs ~¥1,000–2,000 for a drink set. Jimbocho is great for vintage manga, maps, and secondhand books.

**2027-04-19 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Take the train to Harajuku (原宿). Visit Meiji Jingu Shrine (明治神宮) — a serene forested Shinto shrine in the heart of the city, especially beautiful on Sunday mornings when traditional ceremonies may be held. Walk the 1km forested approach path.
- Afternoon: Explore Takeshita Street (竹下通り) for Harajuku's famous youth fashion culture and crepe shops. Then walk through Omotesando (表参道) — Tokyo's most architecturally stunning boulevard, lined with flagship stores designed by world-renowned architects. Visit the Omotesando Hills complex.
- Evening: Head to Shibuya (渋谷) — a 10-minute walk from Omotesando. Experience the legendary Shibuya Scramble Crossing at dusk. Visit the Shibuya Sky observation deck on the rooftop of Scramble Square for stunning 360° sunset views.
- Notes: Meiji Jingu is free to enter. Shibuya Sky tickets: ~¥2,000 — book online. Sunday at Meiji Jingu may feature traditional wedding processions — a wonderful cultural sight. Shibuya Scramble is most impressive at night with neon lights.

**2027-04-20 (Monday)**
- Morning: Visit the teamLab Borderless or teamLab Planets digital art museum (チームラボ) — a world-leading immersive tech-meets-art experience that perfectly bridges Tokyo's tech culture and contemporary art. Book tickets well in advance as they sell out.
- Afternoon: Head to Odaiba (お台場) — Tokyo's futuristic artificial island in Tokyo Bay. Explore DiverCity Tokyo Plaza (home to a life-size Gundam statue), the teamLab area, and the waterfront promenade with views of Rainbow Bridge and the city skyline.
- Evening: Enjoy sunset over Tokyo Bay from Odaiba's waterfront. Take the Yurikamome automated driverless monorail back toward Shiodome — a scenic and uniquely Tokyo transit experience. Dinner near Shiodome or back in Tsukiji.
- Notes: teamLab tickets: ~¥3,200 per person — MUST book online in advance. The Yurikamome monorail offers fantastic views of the bay and is an experience itself. Odaiba is quieter on weekdays — ideal for Monday visit.

**2027-04-21 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Nikko (日光) — a UNESCO World Heritage site about 2 hours from Tokyo by Tobu Limited Express train. Visit the ornate Tosho-gu Shrine (東照宮), one of Japan's most elaborately decorated Shinto shrines, set in a cedar forest in the mountains.
- Afternoon: Continue exploring Nikko — visit Rinno-ji Temple, the Sacred Bridge (Shinkyo), and walk through the forested mountain paths. In spring, cherry blossoms and mountain scenery are spectacular.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo by early evening. Arrive back at Tsukiji by approximately 19:00–20:00. Enjoy a relaxed dinner close to the hotel.
- Notes: Tobu Nikko Pass (2-day) costs ~¥4,780 from Asakusa — includes round-trip train and local bus access. Tosho-ji entry: ~¥1,300. Start early (depart by 7:30–8:00 AM) to maximize time in Nikko. Wear comfortable walking shoes.

**2027-04-22 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit the Nezu Museum (根津美術館) in Omotesando — a beautiful private museum of Japanese and Asian antiquities set in a stunning traditional garden. Excellent for pre-modern Japanese art, ceramics, and calligraphy.
- Afternoon: Explore the Yanaka (谷中) neighborhood — one of Tokyo's best-preserved old shitamachi (downtown) areas. Walk Yanaka Ginza shopping street, browse independent craft shops, visit old temples and cemeteries, and soak in an atmosphere that feels like Tokyo from decades past.
- Evening: Head to Ueno (上野) — visit Ameyoko Market (アメ横) for a lively evening market atmosphere with street food, clothing, and fresh produce. Great for people-watching and snacking.
- Notes: Nezu Museum entry: ~¥1,300, closed Mondays. Yanaka is a wonderful photography location — bring your camera. Yanaka Cemetery is peaceful and beautiful during cherry blossom season. Ameyoko is busiest in the late afternoon and evening.

**2027-04-23 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit the Mori Art Museum (森美術館) in Roppongi Hills — one of Tokyo's premier contemporary art museums with rotating world-class exhibitions, located on the 53rd floor with stunning city views included in admission.
- Afternoon: Explore Roppongi (六本木) — Tokyo's international arts district. Visit the National Art Center Tokyo (国立新美術館) — a stunning Kisho Kurokawa-designed building with free-entry permanent gallery space and rotating exhibitions. Walk between the Roppongi Art Triangle venues.
- Evening: Explore Roppongi's vibrant evening scene — the area transforms after dark. Visit a rooftop bar or Tokyo Tower (東京タワー) which is nearby and beautifully illuminated at night. Walk to Zojo-ji Temple (増上寺) for a dramatic nighttime view of Tokyo Tower framed against the historic gate.
- Notes: Mori Art Museum entry: ~¥2,000 (includes Tokyo City View observation deck). National Art Center is free for permanent collection. Tokyo Tower observation deck: ~¥1,200. Roppongi Art Triangle (Mori + National Art Center + Suntory Museum) can be done over two days if needed.

**2027-04-24 (Friday)**
- Morning: Visit the Edo-Tokyo Museum (江戸東京博物館) in Ryogoku — an outstanding museum covering Tokyo's history from the Edo period to modern day, with life-size reconstructions of old Tokyo streets and bridges. Essential for understanding Tokyo's cultural evolution.
- Afternoon: Explore Ryogoku (両国) — Tokyo's sumo district. Visit the Kokugikan sumo arena (exterior and the small free sumo museum inside). Walk the neighborhood to spot sumo wrestlers from local stables going about their day. Visit Eko-in Memorial and the sumo-themed shops.
- Evening: Head to Kiyosumi-Shirakawa (清澄白河) — Tokyo's coolest emerging neighborhood, known as the 'Brooklyn of Tokyo.' Explore the thriving specialty coffee scene and art gallery district in the early evening.
- Notes: Edo-Tokyo Museum entry: ~¥600. Sumo tournaments (basho) are held in January, May, and September — April is between tournaments, but the museum and neighborhood atmosphere remain excellent. Kiyosumi-Shirakawa is a 15-minute subway ride from Ryogoku.

**2027-04-25 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Kamakura (鎌倉) — about 1 hour from Tokyo by JR Yokosuka Line. Visit the Great Buddha (Kotoku-in Daibutsu) — a massive 13th-century bronze Buddha statue. Explore Hase-dera Temple with its beautiful hillside garden and sea views.
- Afternoon: Walk the Daibutsu Hiking Trail through bamboo groves and forested hills connecting Kamakura's major temples. Visit Tsurugaoka Hachimangu Shrine — Kamakura's most important shrine, set along a grand processional avenue. Browse the charming Komachi Street for local snacks and crafts.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo by early evening. Enjoy a relaxed dinner near the hotel to rest after a full day.
- Notes: JR Pass or Suica card works for this journey. Kamakura is very popular on weekends — depart early (before 8:00 AM) to beat crowds. Great Buddha entry: ~¥300. Hase-dera entry: ~¥400. Wear comfortable walking shoes for the hiking trail.

**2027-04-26 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit the Tokyo National Museum (東京国立博物館) in Ueno — Japan's oldest and largest museum, housing an extraordinary collection of Japanese art, samurai armor, ancient pottery, and Buddhist sculpture. The Honkan (main building) alone is worth several hours.
- Afternoon: Explore Ueno Park (上野公園) — Tokyo's most famous park, home to multiple museums, Shinobazu Pond, and Ueno Zoo. In April, the park may still have late cherry blossoms. Visit Tosho-gu Shrine within the park. Then explore the Akihabara area one more time for any last tech or souvenir shopping.
- Evening: Return to Ginza for a final upscale evening stroll. Visit the Ginza Six rooftop garden (free) for city views. Treat yourself to a special farewell dinner in the Ginza or Tsukiji area.
- Notes: Tokyo National Museum entry: ~¥1,000. Ueno Park is free. Consider this your cultural capstone day. The farewell dinner is a good opportunity to splurge slightly — Ginza has some of Tokyo's finest mid-to-high-end dining within walking distance of the hotel.

**2027-04-27 (Monday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Tokyo — take one last stroll through Tsukiji Outer Market for breakfast and to pick up any last-minute food souvenirs: dried seaweed, premium dashi packs, wasabi, matcha, and Japanese kitchen knives make excellent gifts. Pack and check out of the hotel.
- Afternoon: Depending on departure flight time — if afternoon/evening flight, store luggage at the hotel or a coin locker and spend remaining time in nearby Ginza or Hamarikyu Gardens. Take the Narita Express or Airport Limousine from Tokyo Station or Shimbashi (both nearby) to the airport.
- Evening: Departure from Tokyo. Reflect on 13 extraordinary days exploring the food, tech culture, history, and neighborhoods of one of the world's greatest cities.
- Notes: Allow at least 3 hours before departure for airport transit and check-in. Narita Express takes ~60 minutes from Tokyo Station. Shimbashi Station is a 10-minute walk from the hotel. Use remaining Suica card balance on convenience store purchases at the airport — the card can be kept for future Japan visits or refunded at the airport.
