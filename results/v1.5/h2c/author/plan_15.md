## Day by day

**2027-04-15 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Arrival at airport at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Clear immigration, collect luggage, and take the Narita Express or Limousine Bus to Chiyoda City. Check in to the Chiyoda City Boutique Stay and freshen up.
- Afternoon: Take a gentle orientation walk around the Imperial Palace East Gardens (Higashi Gyoen) — free entry, beautifully maintained, and just minutes from most Chiyoda hotels. Enjoy the cherry blossom aftermath or spring greenery. Stroll along the moat toward Nijubashi Bridge for iconic views.
- Evening: Head to Yurakucho area (5–10 min walk) for your first Tokyo dinner. Explore the atmospheric alleyways under the train tracks filled with yakitori stalls and izakayas. Perfect low-key introduction to Tokyo nightlife.
- Notes: Get a Suica or Pasmo IC card at the airport or hotel area convenience store for seamless train and bus travel throughout the trip. Download Google Maps offline and the Tokyo Metro app.

**2027-04-16 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit the Tsukiji Outer Market (30 min by train or taxi from Chiyoda) — browse fresh seafood stalls, tamagoyaki shops, and specialty food vendors. Arrive by 8:00–9:00 AM for the best experience before crowds peak.
- Afternoon: Walk or take a short train ride to Hamarikyu Gardens — a stunning traditional garden with a teahouse on the water, contrasting beautifully with the surrounding skyscrapers. Afterward, take a water bus up the Sumida River toward Asakusa (¥1,000, scenic ride).
- Evening: Explore Asakusa's Nakamise Shopping Street leading to Senso-ji Temple. The temple is illuminated at night and far less crowded. Browse traditional crafts, ningyo-yaki cakes, and paper fans as souvenirs.
- Notes: Tsukiji Outer Market is best visited early. The inner wholesale market has moved to Toyosu, but the outer market remains a food lover's paradise. Wear comfortable shoes — lots of walking today.

**2027-04-17 (Friday)**
- Morning: Akihabara Electric Town — Tokyo's legendary tech and electronics district, just one stop from central Chiyoda. Explore multi-story electronics stores like Yodobashi Akihabara, retro game shops, and gadget markets. Perfect for tech culture enthusiasts.
- Afternoon: Continue in Akihabara — visit the Radio Kaikan building (8 floors of hobby, anime, and tech goods), explore retro game arcades (Super Potato is a must), and check out maid cafés for a uniquely Tokyo cultural experience.
- Evening: Walk or take a short train to Ueno for dinner, then visit Ueno Park for an evening stroll. Check out Ameya-Yokocho (Ameyoko) market street — a bustling outdoor market with street food, clothing, and local goods.
- Notes: Akihabara is best experienced on a weekday morning when it's less crowded. Many shops open around 10–11 AM. Bring cash — some smaller shops don't accept cards. Ameyoko is liveliest in the early evening.

**2027-04-18 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Nikko (2 hours from Shinjuku/Ueno by Tobu Nikko Line or Shinkansen). Visit the UNESCO World Heritage Tosho-gu Shrine complex — ornate, gold-gilded mausoleums set among ancient cedar forests. Arrive early to beat weekend crowds.
- Afternoon: Explore Rinnoji Temple and Futarasan Shrine within the Nikko shrine complex. Walk the cedar-lined avenue (Sugi Namiki) and visit Shinkyo Sacred Bridge over the Daiya River. Optional: hike toward Kegon Falls (20 min bus ride).
- Evening: Return to Tokyo by early evening. Relax in the hotel or take a quiet walk around the Imperial Palace outer gardens lit by lampposts.
- Notes: Purchase a Tobu Nikko Pass for unlimited travel on the Tobu Line and discounts at attractions (approx. ¥4,000 round trip from Asakusa). Nikko can be cold even in April — bring a light jacket. Book soba dinner early as Yabu Soba closes when they run out.

**2027-04-19 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Harajuku and Meiji Jingu Shrine — start with the serene forested shrine, a spiritual oasis in the heart of the city. The torii gate entrance and gravel path through ancient trees is deeply atmospheric on a Sunday morning.
- Afternoon: Walk down Takeshita Street for Harajuku's famous youth fashion and quirky street food (crepes, cotton candy, rainbow toast). Then shift to the adjacent Omotesando avenue for upscale boutiques, concept stores, and architectural gems like Prada and Louis Vuitton buildings.
- Evening: Head to Shibuya — experience the world-famous Shibuya Scramble Crossing at dusk. Visit the Shibuya Sky observation deck (¥2,000) for panoramic sunset views over the city. Explore Shibuya's vibrant restaurant and bar scene.
- Notes: Shibuya Scramble is most dramatic at dusk or night. The Shibuya Sky deck is best booked online in advance. Sunday in Harajuku means cosplay performers near the park entrance — a great photo opportunity.

**2027-04-20 (Monday)**
- Morning: Odaiba — futuristic waterfront island accessible by the driverless Yurikamome monorail (scenic ride itself). Visit teamLab Borderless or teamLab Planets (book tickets well in advance) — immersive digital art installations that sit at the intersection of tech and culture.
- Afternoon: Explore Odaiba's DiverCity Tokyo Plaza (life-size Gundam statue outside), the retro-futuristic Palette Town area, and the Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation (Miraikan) — Japan's premier science museum with robots, space tech, and AI exhibits.
- Evening: Catch the sunset view of Rainbow Bridge and the Tokyo skyline from Odaiba's waterfront promenade. Return to central Tokyo via the Yurikamome or water bus.
- Notes: teamLab tickets MUST be booked online in advance — they frequently sell out weeks ahead. Miraikan is excellent for tech enthusiasts and has English displays throughout. The Yurikamome monorail is worth the ride for views alone.

**2027-04-21 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Shinjuku — start at the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building (free observation deck, opens 9:30 AM) for panoramic city views including Mt. Fuji on clear days. Explore the surrounding Nishi-Shinjuku skyscraper district, a showcase of Japanese corporate architecture.
- Afternoon: Cross to East Shinjuku — explore Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (one of Japan's finest parks, ¥500 entry). Afterward, wander through Kabukicho entertainment district and the compact, atmospheric Golden Gai bar alley.
- Evening: Experience Shinjuku at night — the neon-lit Kabukicho area, Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) with tiny smoky yakitori stalls, and the energy of one of the world's busiest train stations.
- Notes: Golden Gai bars are tiny (some seat only 6 people) and some have cover charges of ¥500–¥1,000. They are welcoming to tourists. Shinjuku Gyoen closes Mondays — confirmed open Tuesday. The observation deck offers the best Mt. Fuji views on clear winter/spring mornings.

**2027-04-22 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Kamakura (1 hour from Tokyo by JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo Station). Start at the Great Buddha of Kotoku-in (Kamakura Daibutsu) — a 13-meter bronze statue set against forested hills. Explore Hase-dera Temple with its sea views and cave shrines.
- Afternoon: Walk the Daibutsu Hiking Trail through bamboo groves and forest to Zeniarai Benten Shrine (a cave shrine where you wash money for good fortune). Descend into Kamakura town and explore Komachi-dori shopping street for local snacks and crafts.
- Evening: Return to Tokyo. Optional stop at Yokohama's Chinatown (en route) for dinner before heading back to Chiyoda.
- Notes: Use a JR Pass (if purchased) or buy a single ticket from Tokyo Station. The Kamakura–Enoshima Pass (¥700) covers local Enoden tram rides. The hiking trail takes 60–90 minutes and requires sturdy shoes. Avoid rainy days for this excursion.

**2027-04-23 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Yanaka District — Tokyo's best-preserved old shitamachi (downtown) neighborhood. Wander through Yanaka Ginza shopping street, explore the atmospheric cemetery (where many famous Japanese figures are buried), and browse independent craft shops and galleries.
- Afternoon: Continue through the Nezu and Yanesen area. Visit Nezu Shrine — a smaller, less-visited alternative to Fushimi Inari with rows of torii gates and seasonal flowers. Walk to the nearby Scai the Bathhouse — a contemporary art gallery in a converted 200-year-old bathhouse.
- Evening: Head to Akihabara (short train ride) for a second, more focused visit — specifically for retro gaming, anime culture, or any tech items you missed. End the evening with dinner in the Kanda area.
- Notes: Yanaka is one of the few areas that survived WWII bombing and the 1923 earthquake, preserving Edo-period urban character. Many shops are closed on Wednesdays — confirmed open Thursday. Bring cash as many small shops are cash-only.

**2027-04-24 (Friday)**
- Morning: Roppongi — visit the Mori Art Museum (52nd floor of Mori Tower, ¥2,000) for contemporary Japanese and international art with stunning city views from the adjacent observation deck. Roppongi is Tokyo's art district with world-class galleries.
- Afternoon: Walk to the National Art Center Tokyo (free entry to building, exhibition fees vary) — Japan's largest exhibition space with rotating major shows. Nearby 21_21 Design Sight (by Tadao Ando) offers design-focused exhibitions.
- Evening: Roppongi Hills or Tokyo Midtown for evening dining and drinks. These upscale complexes have excellent mid-range dining options and are beautifully lit at night. Explore the outdoor art installations in Midtown Garden.
- Notes: The Mori Art Museum is open until 22:00 on Fridays — ideal for a late start. The observation deck (Tokyo City View) is included with the museum ticket. Roppongi has a vibrant nightlife scene if you want to extend your evening.

**2027-04-25 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Toyosu Market — the world's largest wholesale fish market (replaced Tsukiji's inner market). Book the observation area tour in advance to watch the famous tuna auction (lottery system, very limited). Explore the market buildings and seafood restaurants.
- Afternoon: Ginza — Tokyo's most prestigious shopping and cultural district, a short distance from Chiyoda. Visit the Itoya stationery flagship store (9 floors, a Japanese cultural institution), the Sony Park interactive experience, and browse the Ginza Six complex.
- Evening: Ginza at night is elegant and walkable. Visit the Okuno Building — a 1932 art deco building housing dozens of tiny artist studios and galleries open to visitors. End with a stroll along Chuo-dori (car-free on weekends).
- Notes: Tuna auctions at Toyosu require advance lottery registration on the Toyosu Market website — apply well before the trip. The observation area (no auction) is easier to access. Ginza's Chuo-dori becomes a pedestrian zone on weekend afternoons — great for strolling.

**2027-04-26 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Shimokitazawa — Tokyo's bohemian neighborhood known for vintage clothing, indie music venues, and a creative, youthful atmosphere. Browse the dozens of thrift and vintage stores, independent bookshops, and record stores. A window into Tokyo's alternative culture scene.
- Afternoon: Head to Nakameguro — walk along the Meguro River (lined with cherry trees, beautiful even after bloom season with fresh green leaves). Explore the trendy boutiques, cafés, and design shops along the riverbank. Visit the Daikanyama T-Site bookstore complex — an architectural and cultural landmark.
- Evening: Ebisu and Daikanyama for a relaxed final full-evening dinner. These upscale but approachable neighborhoods have excellent restaurants, wine bars, and craft beer spots.
- Notes: Shimokitazawa is best explored slowly and spontaneously — don't over-plan. Many vintage stores open around 11–12 AM. Daikanyama T-Site is as much a lifestyle concept as a bookstore — the architecture and curation are worth experiencing even without buying.

**2027-04-27 (Monday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Tokyo — revisit a favorite neighborhood or spot you want to see one more time. Alternatively, visit the Edo-Tokyo Museum in Ryogoku (if not already visited) for a comprehensive look at Tokyo's history from the Edo period to modern day. Or spend a peaceful final morning at the Imperial Palace East Gardens.
- Afternoon: Last-minute shopping and souvenir gathering — return to Akihabara for tech goods, Ginza for premium Japanese crafts, or Asakusa for traditional souvenirs. Don't forget: Japanese Kit-Kat flavors, wagashi sweets, and stationery from Itoya make excellent gifts.
- Evening: Pack, check out, and transfer to the airport. Allow 2–2.5 hours before departure. Grab a final convenience store onigiri or airport ramen for a true Tokyo farewell.
- Notes: Narita Airport is 60–90 minutes from central Tokyo (Narita Express from Tokyo Station, ¥3,070). Haneda is closer at 30–40 minutes. Confirm your departure airport. Remember to claim consumption tax refunds at the airport tax refund counter for eligible purchases made in Japan.
