## Day by day

**2027-11-15 (Sunday)**
- Morning: No activities scheduled — flight LH arrives at 13:00. Rest and travel time.
- Afternoon: Arrive at Queenstown Airport at 13:00. Transfer to Queenstown Boutique Stay, check in, freshen up, and take a leisurely stroll along the Queenstown lakefront promenade to get oriented. Enjoy views of Lake Wakatipu and the Remarkables mountain range.
- Evening: Explore central Queenstown on foot — browse the waterfront, visit Steamer Wharf, and get your bearings around town. Light evening walk up to the lakefront viewpoint near Marine Parade.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light — jet lag may be a factor. Pick up a NZ SIM card at the airport. Confirm all activity bookings for the week ahead. Weather in November is late spring — pack layers.

**2027-11-16 (Monday)**
- Morning: Nevis Bungy Jump — New Zealand's highest bungy at 134 meters above the Nevis River gorge with AJ Hackett. Depart from the AJ Hackett booking station in central Queenstown at 8:30 AM. Transfer by 4WD to the platform takes about 30 minutes.
- Afternoon: Return to Queenstown by early afternoon. Recover and recharge with a relaxed walk along the Queenstown Gardens — a peaceful lakeside park perfect for post-adrenaline unwinding. Explore the rose garden and foreshore trails.
- Evening: Head up the Skyline Gondola at sunset for panoramic views over Lake Wakatipu and the Remarkables. Watch the sun set over the mountains from the observation deck.
- Notes: Book the Nevis Bungy well in advance — it sells out fast. Weight and health restrictions apply; check AJ Hackett's website. Minimum age 10, minimum weight 45kg. Video package available as an add-on (~NZD 50).

**2027-11-17 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Routeburn Track Guided Day Walk — depart Queenstown early (around 7:00 AM) via shuttle to the Routeburn trailhead. This stunning alpine hike passes through Fiordland and Mount Aspiring National Parks with beech forests, waterfalls, and mountain panoramas.
- Afternoon: Continue the Routeburn walk through the afternoon, reaching the alpine meadows and viewpoints near Routeburn Flats or Routeburn Falls hut. The guided experience includes interpretation of local flora, fauna, and Māori history.
- Evening: Return shuttle to Queenstown arriving approximately 6:00–7:00 PM. Rest at the boutique stay, shower, and enjoy a quieter evening in town.
- Notes: Wear sturdy hiking boots and moisture-wicking layers. The Routeburn is a multi-day Great Walk but guided day versions cover the most scenic sections. Total walking distance on the day walk is approximately 16–20 km. Trekking poles recommended.

**2027-11-18 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Active rest day morning — gentle hike up Ben Lomond Track from Queenstown town centre. The lower section to the saddle is achievable in 2–3 hours return and offers sweeping views over the lake and town without the full summit commitment.
- Afternoon: Visit Arrowtown, a charming historic gold-rush village 20 minutes from Queenstown. Explore the Chinese Settlement, Arrow River gorge walk, and browse the boutique shops and galleries on Buckingham Street.
- Evening: Return to Queenstown. Enjoy the vibrant bar scene around the waterfront — try Zephyr or Atlas Beer Cafe for craft New Zealand beers and live music.
- Notes: Arrowtown is a great half-day detour and can be reached by car rental, local shuttle, or taxi. The Arrow River walk from town is flat and easy — good for tired legs. November daylight lasts until around 9:00 PM.

**2027-11-19 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Drive or shuttle from Queenstown to Te Anau (approximately 2.5 hours). Check in to a Te Anau accommodation or leave bags at storage. Visit the Te Anau Glowworm Caves — a boat trip across the lake leads to an underground world of glowworms and limestone formations.
- Afternoon: Explore the Te Anau lakefront and surrounding trails. Walk the easy Kepler Track day section near the control gates — a flat, scenic lakeside trail through beech forest perfect for a gentle afternoon hike.
- Evening: Settle into Te Anau for the night — this is the gateway town for Milford Sound and an overnight stay is essential for an early Milford departure.
- Notes: Book Te Anau accommodation in advance — the town is small and fills up quickly in summer. The glowworm cave tour must be pre-booked through Real Journeys (~NZD 85 per person). Drive from Queenstown to Te Anau is scenic via the Mossburn route.

**2027-11-20 (Friday)**
- Morning: Milford Sound Kayak and Walk Combo — depart Te Anau at 6:30 AM for the 2-hour scenic drive through Fiordland National Park to Milford Sound. Begin the kayaking portion in the morning — paddle through the iconic fiord beneath Mitre Peak with towering cliffs and cascading waterfalls all around.
- Afternoon: Complete the walk component of the combo — hike a section of the Milford Track foreshore trail. Enjoy views of Stirling Falls and the fiord from land. Optional cruise on the fiord if time permits.
- Evening: Return drive to Te Anau (2 hours). Rest and reflect on one of New Zealand's most iconic landscapes.
- Notes: The Milford Sound road (SH94) is one of the most scenic drives in the world — stop at the Mirror Lakes, Eglinton Valley, and Homer Tunnel viewpoints. Check road conditions before departure as the Homer Tunnel can close due to rockfall or avalanche risk in early season. Wet weather gear is essential — Milford receives over 7 meters of rain annually.

**2027-11-21 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Drive from Te Anau to Wanaka (approximately 2.5 hours via Queenstown or 3 hours via the Crown Range). Stop at Cardrona for photos and a coffee at the historic Cardrona Hotel. Arrive in Wanaka and check in or drop bags.
- Afternoon: Roy's Peak Track — one of Wanaka's signature hikes. The 16 km return trail climbs steeply to 1,578 meters with jaw-dropping views over Lake Wanaka and the Southern Alps. Allow 5–6 hours for the full hike.
- Evening: Return to Wanaka township. Enjoy the laid-back lakefront atmosphere — far less crowded than Queenstown. Stroll along the lake edge and visit the famous 'That Wanaka Tree' at sunset.
- Notes: Roy's Peak is steep and exposed — start early to avoid the midday heat and afternoon crowds. Carry 2+ liters of water, sunscreen, and layers. The track is well-marked but can be slippery if wet. No shade on the upper sections. Wanaka is a great alternative base to Queenstown — quieter and equally beautiful.

**2027-11-22 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Active rest morning in Wanaka — visit Puzzling World for a fun cultural break, or explore the Outlet Track, a flat 3 km lakeside walk from the town centre to the lake outlet. Perfect for a gentle morning after Roy's Peak.
- Afternoon: Drive to Haast Pass (1.5 hours from Wanaka) — one of New Zealand's most dramatic mountain passes. Walk the short Fantail Falls Track (20 minutes return) and Thunder Creek Falls Track (10 minutes return) along the way. Continue to the West Coast if time allows.
- Evening: Return to Wanaka or continue to Fox Glacier for an overnight stay if the itinerary calls for a West Coast extension.
- Notes: The Haast Pass road (SH6) is stunning but can be affected by slips and flooding — check NZTA road conditions. The West Coast is a significant detour; only extend if time and energy allow. November weather on the West Coast can be unpredictable — always carry rain gear.

**2027-11-23 (Monday)**
- Morning: Drive from Wanaka back to Queenstown (1.5 hours via Crown Range — the highest sealed road in New Zealand with incredible views). Stop at the Crown Range summit lookout. Return to Queenstown Boutique Stay and check back in.
- Afternoon: Queenstown Hill Time Walk — a 2–3 hour return hike directly from the town centre up to the summit of Queenstown Hill (Te Tapu-nui). Excellent 360-degree views over the lake, Remarkables, and surrounding ranges. A hidden gem compared to Ben Lomond.
- Evening: Celebrate the halfway point of the trip with a nice dinner and a walk along the waterfront. Visit the Steamer Wharf area for evening atmosphere.
- Notes: The Crown Range road is steep and winding — take it slow, especially in wet conditions. It is not recommended for campervans or large vehicles. Queenstown Hill walk is dog-friendly and very popular with locals — go early or late to avoid crowds.

**2027-11-24 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Jet boat on the Shotover River — the iconic Shotover Jet departs from Arthurs Point, 10 minutes from Queenstown. 25-minute ride through narrow canyon walls at high speed with 360-degree spins. A classic Queenstown adrenaline experience.
- Afternoon: Gibbston Valley wine region — hire a bike or join a tour to explore the wineries of the 'Valley of the Vines'. Gibbston Valley Winery, Peregrine, and Chard Farm all offer tastings. The area is 20 minutes from Queenstown by car.
- Evening: Return to Queenstown. Relaxed evening with a riverside walk along the Kawarau River near Frankton.
- Notes: Shotover Jet must be pre-booked (~NZD 169 per person). Gibbston Valley is Central Otago's premium wine region — Pinot Noir is the star variety. If cycling between wineries, the Gibbston River Trail is a flat, scenic 10 km trail. Designated driver or shuttle recommended for wine tasting.

**2027-11-25 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Day trip to Hanmer Springs Thermal Pools — drive from Queenstown north via SH8 and SH7 (approximately 3.5–4 hours). This is a longer drive but the destination offers perfect recovery for tired hiking legs. Arrive mid-morning.
- Afternoon: Hanmer Springs Thermal Pools & Spa — soak in multiple geothermal pools of varying temperatures surrounded by mountain scenery. Then explore the Hanmer Forest Park with a gentle walk through the Conical Hill Loop Track (1.5 hours, easy) through exotic and native forest.
- Evening: Overnight in Hanmer Springs or begin the drive back toward Christchurch for the following days.
- Notes: Hanmer Springs Thermal Pools entry is ~NZD 30–45 per person depending on pools accessed. The drive from Queenstown is long — consider an overnight stay in Hanmer Springs to make it worthwhile. Alternatively, this day can be swapped to a Christchurch transit day if the drive feels too ambitious.

**2027-11-26 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Drive from Hanmer Springs to Christchurch (1.5 hours) or return toward Queenstown depending on departure logistics. If heading to Christchurch, stop at Kaikōura on the coast (1.5 hours north of Hanmer) for a short walk along the Kaikōura Peninsula Walkway with seal colony viewing.
- Afternoon: Explore Christchurch — visit the Botanic Gardens, punting on the Avon River, and the Re:START container mall. Walk through the rebuilt city centre and visit the stunning Cardboard Cathedral.
- Evening: Final evening on the South Island — enjoy a farewell dinner in Christchurch's vibrant dining precinct around Victoria Street and the Oxford Terrace.
- Notes: Kaikōura is famous for its marine wildlife — whale watching tours are available but require advance booking and a half-day commitment. The peninsula walkway seal colony is free and takes about 2 hours. Christchurch is the main South Island hub for return flights — confirm flight details for the following day.

**2027-11-27 (Friday)**
- Morning: Final morning in New Zealand — if departing from Christchurch, enjoy a leisurely breakfast and a last walk through the Botanic Gardens or along the Avon River. If departing from Queenstown, allow 3.5 hours travel time back from Christchurch.
- Afternoon: Transfer to Christchurch Airport or Queenstown Airport depending on departure routing. Allow extra time for baggage check-in and security. Pick up last-minute New Zealand souvenirs — Whittaker's chocolate, Manuka honey, and merino wool items are popular choices.
- Evening: Depart New Zealand — farewell to the South Island. Reflect on 13 incredible days of hiking, adventure, and natural beauty.
- Notes: Christchurch Airport is approximately 15–20 minutes from the city centre. Queenstown Airport is 10 minutes from the town centre. Check baggage allowances for any outdoor gear purchased or rented. New Zealand has strict biosecurity — declare all food items and ensure hiking boots are clean. No departure tax is separately charged (included in airfare).
