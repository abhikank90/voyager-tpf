## Day by day

**2027-12-15 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: N/A – In transit. Flight LH lands at Incheon International Airport at 13:00.
- Afternoon: Clear immigration and customs at Incheon Airport, collect luggage, and take the AREX Express Train (~43 min) to Seoul Station, then transfer by subway or taxi to your boutique stay near Gyeongbokgung. Check in, freshen up, and take a gentle orientation stroll around Bukchon Hanok Village – a preserved traditional Korean village just minutes from the hotel. Admire the hanok rooftops and narrow alleys at a leisurely pace.
- Evening: Explore the lively Insadong street market area (10-min walk), browsing artisan shops and souvenir stalls. Settle in for a warming first dinner featuring Korean classics.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light – jet lag may set in. Exchange some currency at the airport (Incheon has competitive rates) or use a Wise/Revolut card. Pick up a T-money transit card at the airport or any convenience store.

**2027-12-16 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Visit Gyeongbokgung Palace (경복궁) – one of Korea's grandest Joseon-era palaces, right on your doorstep. Arrive early (~9:00) to catch the Royal Guard Changing Ceremony (held on the hour). Consider renting a hanbok (traditional Korean dress) from a nearby rental shop (~₩15,000–20,000/2 hrs) for free entry to the palace and beautiful photo opportunities.
- Afternoon: Explore the National Folk Museum of Korea located inside the palace grounds (free with palace ticket). Afterward, walk north to Bukchon Hanok Village for a more in-depth self-guided tour, then head to Samcheongdong-gil street for indie cafés, galleries, and boutique shops.
- Evening: Head to Gwanghwamun Square to see the statue of Admiral Yi Sun-sin illuminated at night. Stroll the area and enjoy the winter city lights.
- Notes: Gyeongbokgung Palace ticket: ₩3,000. Dress warmly – December in Seoul is cold (0°C to -5°C typical). Hand warmers from convenience stores are very useful.

**2027-12-17 (Thursday)**
- Morning: Visit Changdeokgung Palace (창덕궁) and its UNESCO-listed Secret Garden (Huwon). Book the Secret Garden tour in advance online (~₩8,000). The winter landscape of bare trees and frozen ponds is hauntingly beautiful. The palace is a 15-min walk or short subway ride from the hotel.
- Afternoon: Walk to Changgyeonggung Palace next door (₩1,000 entry) for a quieter, less-touristy palace experience. Then head to Ikseon-dong Hanok Village – a trendy neighborhood of converted hanoks now housing hip cafés, cocktail bars, and fusion eateries.
- Evening: Explore Ikseon-dong's nighttime ambiance – string lights and warm café glow make it magical in winter. Browse unique Korean design shops and pick up handcrafted goods.
- Notes: Secret Garden tours are in Korean but English audio guides are available. Book tickets at least a day ahead via the official Changdeokgung website.

**2027-12-18 (Friday)**
- Morning: Take the subway to Insadong (인사동) for a deep dive into Korean traditional arts and crafts. Visit Ssamziegil, an open-air courtyard mall with indie Korean designers, handmade goods, and street performers. Browse antique shops and calligraphy studios along the main street.
- Afternoon: Head to Jogyesa Temple (조계사) – the head temple of Korean Buddhism, just a short walk from Insadong. In December, it's often decorated with stunning lanterns. Then visit the nearby Unhyeongung Palace, a smaller royal residence with a more intimate feel (~₩1,000 entry).
- Evening: Walk to Cheonggyecheon Stream (청계천) – a beautifully lit urban stream perfect for an evening stroll. In winter, the stream is often decorated with light festivals. End the night in the Euljiro area, a former industrial district now buzzing with trendy bars and pojangmacha (street food tents).
- Notes: Jogyesa Temple is free to enter and open daily. The Cheonggyecheon Light Festival (if running in December 2027) is a highlight – check the Seoul Tourism website for exact dates.

**2027-12-19 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Day trip to the DMZ (Demilitarized Zone) – book a half-day or full-day guided tour departing from central Seoul (~₩50,000–80,000 including transport). Visit the 3rd Infiltration Tunnel, Dora Observatory, and Dorasan Station. A sobering and deeply educational K-culture experience.
- Afternoon: Return to Seoul by mid-afternoon. Rest briefly at the hotel, then head to Hongdae (홍대) neighborhood – Seoul's youth culture and K-indie music hub. Explore the street art, browse vinyl record shops, and check out K-fashion boutiques.
- Evening: Experience Hongdae's famous Saturday night street performances near the main park. Catch live busking – K-pop covers, indie bands, and dance crews perform regularly. Dive into the nightlife at a craft beer bar or norebang (karaoke room) for a quintessential Korean evening.
- Notes: Book the DMZ tour at least 2–3 days in advance as they fill up quickly, especially on weekends. Bring your passport – it's required for entry. Norebang rooms in Hongdae cost ~₩15,000–25,000/hour for a private room.

**2027-12-20 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Visit N Seoul Tower (남산타워) on Namsan Mountain. Take the cable car up (~₩15,000 round trip) or hike the Namsan trail (free, ~40 min). Enjoy panoramic winter views over Seoul's skyline. Browse the famous love lock fence and visit the observatory (~₩21,000).
- Afternoon: Descend to Myeongdong (명동) – Seoul's most famous shopping and street food district. Browse Korean beauty (K-beauty) flagship stores: Innisfree, Etude House, Olive Young. Stock up on skincare, sheet masks, and cosmetics at mid-range prices.
- Evening: Explore Myeongdong's famous outdoor street food market – dozens of stalls line the pedestrian streets. Try multiple snacks and end the evening at a traditional Korean restaurant nearby.
- Notes: Myeongdong is extremely busy on Sundays. Go to Olive Young for the best K-beauty deals – prices are cheaper here than at airport duty-free for most items. Many vendors accept card payments.

**2027-12-21 (Monday)**
- Morning: Visit the National Museum of Korea (국립중앙박물관) in Yongsan – one of Asia's largest museums, free entry. Explore 12,000 years of Korean history across beautifully curated galleries. Highlights include the Silla gold crowns, Goryeo celadon ceramics, and the grand stone pagoda in the central hall.
- Afternoon: Walk through the adjacent Yongsan Family Park, then head to Itaewon (이태원) – Seoul's most internationally diverse neighborhood. Explore Antique Furniture Street, Haebangchon (HBC) village for indie cafés, and the eclectic mix of global restaurants and boutiques.
- Evening: Itaewon has a vibrant nightlife scene. Visit a rooftop bar with Han River views or explore the Noksapyeong area for craft cocktail bars. Alternatively, visit the War Memorial of Korea (free, closes at 6 PM) if interested in modern Korean history.
- Notes: National Museum of Korea is closed on Mondays – if so, swap this activity with the War Memorial of Korea (open Tuesdays–Sundays) or visit Leeum Samsung Museum of Art in Itaewon instead (~₩10,000 entry).

**2027-12-22 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Explore Gangnam (강남) district – take the subway south of the Han River. Visit COEX Mall, one of Asia's largest underground shopping centers, and the stunning Starfield Library inside (a free, photogenic public library with floor-to-ceiling bookshelves). Browse the adjacent Bongeunsa Temple (봉은사) – a tranquil Buddhist temple amid Gangnam's skyscrapers.
- Afternoon: Walk Garosu-gil (가로수길) – a tree-lined boulevard in Sinsa-dong with high-end Korean fashion boutiques, concept stores, and artisan coffee shops. Then head to Apgujeong Rodeo Street for a glimpse into Seoul's luxury K-fashion scene and celebrity culture.
- Evening: Head back toward the Han River and visit Banpo Bridge Rainbow Fountain (반포대교 달빛무지개분수) – the world's longest bridge fountain, lit up beautifully at night. Walk along Banpo Hangang Park for riverside views.
- Notes: Bongeunsa Temple is free to enter. The Banpo Bridge fountain show schedule varies by season – check the Seoul Metropolitan Government website for December 2027 showtimes (typically evenings on weekends).

**2027-12-23 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Take a day trip to Suwon Hwaseong Fortress (수원화성) – a UNESCO World Heritage Site, ~1 hour from Seoul by subway (Line 1 to Suwon Station). Walk the 5.7km fortress wall circuit for stunning views of the city. The fortress is especially beautiful in winter light.
- Afternoon: Visit Haenggung Palace inside the fortress grounds. Explore Paldalmun Gate and the surrounding traditional market. Browse Suwon's famous local food stalls near the fortress entrance.
- Evening: Return to Seoul. Relax at the hotel or explore the neighborhood around Gyeongbokgung at night – the palace walls are beautifully lit and the streets are quiet and atmospheric.
- Notes: Hwaseong Fortress entry: ~₩1,000. The fortress is partially illuminated at night but visiting during daylight is recommended for the full experience. Tongin Market closes in the early evening – plan accordingly.

**2027-12-24 (Thursday – Christmas Eve)**
- Morning: Visit Jogyesa Temple again for morning Buddhist ceremonies, or explore the Seochon Village (서촌) – the charming neighborhood west of Gyeongbokgung, full of independent bookstores, art studios, and traditional tea houses. This quieter alternative to Bukchon is beloved by locals.
- Afternoon: Head to Lotte World Tower (롯데월드타워) in Jamsil – take the elevator to the Seoul Sky Observatory on the 117th–123rd floors for breathtaking city views (~₩29,000). On Christmas Eve, the area is festively decorated and magical.
- Evening: Christmas Eve in Seoul is a major event – Koreans celebrate it like Valentine's Day. Head to Myeongdong Cathedral (명동성당) for the midnight mass atmosphere and beautiful Christmas decorations. The streets of Myeongdong are packed with festive energy, lights, and seasonal street food.
- Notes: Christmas Eve is extremely busy in Seoul. Book dinner reservations well in advance. Myeongdong Cathedral holds a midnight mass open to the public – a beautiful cultural experience even for non-Christians.

**2027-12-25 (Friday – Christmas Day)**
- Morning: Enjoy a slower Christmas morning. Visit Gyeongbokgung Palace one more time at opening (9:00 AM) for a peaceful, crowd-free experience in the crisp winter morning. The palace in snow (if it falls) is extraordinarily beautiful. Take your time with the architecture and the National Palace Museum of Korea inside (free).
- Afternoon: Head to the Han River (한강) – rent a bicycle along the riverside path (Yeouido Hangang Park is popular) or simply walk and enjoy the winter scenery. Visit the 63 Building or take a Han River cruise (~₩15,000–20,000) for scenic city views from the water.
- Evening: Explore Yeouido (여의도) – Seoul's financial district turned weekend leisure hub. Visit the IFC Mall for upscale shopping or catch a movie at CGV IMAX. Many Korean families celebrate Christmas with fried chicken and beer – join the tradition!
- Notes: Many museums and attractions are open on Christmas Day in South Korea (it is a public holiday). The Han River parks are popular with locals on holidays. Dress in layers – riverside wind chill can be significant in December.

**2027-12-26 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Take a day trip to Nami Island (남이섬) and Petite France, ~1.5 hours from Seoul by train + ferry. Nami Island is a beautiful tree-lined island famous for its K-drama filming locations (Winter Sonata). In December, the bare trees and potential snow create a fairy-tale atmosphere.
- Afternoon: Visit Petite France (쁘띠프랑스) – a French-themed cultural village on a hillside near Nami Island, also famous as a K-drama filming location. Browse the colorful buildings and enjoy the scenic valley views. Return to Seoul by early evening.
- Evening: Back in Seoul, head to Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP, 동대문디자인플라자) – Zaha Hadid's futuristic silver landmark. The building is illuminated dramatically at night. Browse the adjacent Dongdaemun Market, one of the world's largest fashion wholesale markets, open until dawn.
- Notes: Nami Island entry: ~₩16,000 (includes ferry). Book the ITX-Cheongchun train from Yongsan Station to Gapyeong Station in advance. The Nami Island + Petite France combo tour can also be booked through operators like KKday or Viator (~₩50,000–60,000 including transport).

**2027-12-27 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Final morning in Seoul – revisit your favorite neighborhood for last-minute shopping and souvenirs. Insadong is ideal for traditional gifts: hanji paper goods, celadon pottery, Korean teas, and packaged traditional sweets (hangwa). Alternatively, visit a large supermarket (Lotte Mart or Emart) for food souvenirs: ramen packs, seaweed snacks, gochujang paste, and Korean instant coffee.
- Afternoon: Check out of the hotel (store luggage if needed). Take a final stroll through Gyeongbokgung's exterior walls and Gwanghwamun Gate for farewell photos. Head to the airport with ample time – AREX Express from Seoul Station to Incheon takes ~43 minutes (trains run frequently). Allow at least 3 hours before departure for check-in and security.
- Evening: Departure from Incheon International Airport. Browse duty-free shops for last-minute K-beauty deals (Sulwhasoo, Laneige, COSRX). Enjoy a final Korean meal at the airport before boarding.
- Notes: Pack souvenirs carefully – Korean ramen and liquids (gochujang, soy sauce) must go in checked luggage. Keep K-beauty items and small packaged foods in carry-on if under liquid limits. The AREX Express from Seoul Station is the fastest and most reliable airport transfer option. Have a wonderful journey home!
