## Day by day

**2027-05-15 (Saturday)**
- Morning: Arrival at Lisbon Airport at 13:00 — no morning activities scheduled. Clear customs, collect luggage, and take the Aerobus or Metro (Red Line to Green Line) to reach Lisbon City Stay. Check in, freshen up, and get oriented with a neighborhood walk around your accommodation.
- Afternoon: Head to the Time Out Market Lisbon (Mercado da Ribeira) for a self-guided food tour. Sample Portuguese classics like bacalhau croquettes, bifanas, fresh seafood, and local wines. This iconic food hall is perfect for a first taste of Lisbon's culinary scene without committing to a full sit-down meal — ideal after a long travel morning.
- Evening: Stroll along the Ribeira waterfront promenade at sunset, then explore the Cais do Sodré neighborhood. Enjoy a relaxed dinner at a nearby tasca (traditional tavern) in the area.
- Notes: Keep Day 1 light and flexible to recover from travel. The Time Out Market is a 10-minute walk or short Uber from most central Lisbon hotels. Pick up a Lisboa Card at the airport or hotel for unlimited metro/tram access over the coming days.

**2027-05-16 (Sunday)**
- Morning: Start the day with a traditional Portuguese breakfast at a local café near the hotel — coffee (uma bica) and a fresh pastel de nata. Then join the Alfama District Walking Tour, exploring Lisbon's oldest and most atmospheric neighborhood. Wind through narrow cobblestone streets, visit the Miradouro de Santa Luzia viewpoint, and learn about the city's Moorish and medieval heritage. The tour typically runs 2–3 hours.
- Afternoon: After the tour, visit the São Jorge Castle (Castelo de São Jorge) perched above Alfama for panoramic views over Lisbon and the Tagus River. Explore the ramparts, towers, and the small archaeological site within. Spend 1.5–2 hours here before descending back into Alfama for a rest.
- Evening: Return to Alfama for the Fado Show component of the experience. Many Alfama restaurants offer Fado dinner shows starting around 20:00. Book a table at a mid-range Fado house such as Tasca do Chico or Sr. Fado for an authentic performance with dinner included.
- Notes: Wear comfortable walking shoes — Alfama's cobblestone streets are steep and uneven. Tram 28 passes through Alfama and is a scenic (if crowded) way to get there, or take a short Uber. Book the Fado dinner show at least 1–2 days ahead.

**2027-05-17 (Monday)**
- Morning: Take the train from Cais do Sodré station to Belém (approximately 15 minutes, included with Lisboa Card). Begin with a visit to the UNESCO-listed Jerónimos Monastery, a stunning example of Manueline Gothic architecture built in the 16th century. Allow 1.5 hours to explore the church, cloisters, and royal tombs of Vasco da Gama and Luís de Camões.
- Afternoon: Walk along the waterfront to the iconic Tower of Belém (Torre de Belém), another Manueline masterpiece and symbol of Portugal's Age of Discovery. Afterwards, visit the nearby Monument to the Discoveries (Padrão dos Descobrimentos) for additional historical context. End the afternoon with a visit to the Museu de Arte Popular or simply relax by the Tagus.
- Evening: Stop at the original Pastéis de Belém bakery (open since 1837) for the world-famous custard tarts fresh from the oven — a non-negotiable Lisbon experience. Return to central Lisbon for dinner in the Príncipe Real or Bairro Alto neighborhood.
- Notes: Belém is very popular; arrive at the Jerónimos Monastery right when it opens (10:00) to beat the crowds. Pre-book tickets online. The Pastéis de Belém bakery can have a queue — it moves quickly and is absolutely worth the wait. Belém is a 15-min train ride, making it an easy full-day excursion.

**2027-05-18 (Tuesday)**
- Morning: Join the Mouraria & Intendente Neighborhood Food & History Walk. Discover Lisbon's multicultural heart in the historic Mouraria quarter — the birthplace of Fado — with its mix of Portuguese, African, and Asian influences. The guided walk covers the history of the Moorish quarter, the Intendente square, local market stalls, and food tastings along the way.
- Afternoon: Visit the National Museum of Ancient Art (Museu Nacional de Arte Antiga) in the Lapa neighborhood. Explore Portugal's most important art collection, featuring masterpieces from the 12th to 19th centuries including the famous Panels of São Vicente de Fora and works by Hieronymus Bosch. Allow 2–2.5 hours. Afterwards, take a short walk through the elegant Chiado neighborhood, browsing its bookshops, boutiques, and cafés.
- Evening: Enjoy the Chiado area at dusk — visit the iconic A Brasileira café (a literary institution since 1905) for a coffee, then head to a rooftop bar such as Park Bar (above a parking garage in Bairro Alto) for sundowner drinks with city views before dinner.
- Notes: The Mouraria walk is best done in the morning when the neighborhood's market and local life are most active. The National Museum of Ancient Art is closed on Mondays, so Tuesday is ideal. Chiado is walkable from the museum via tram or Uber (10–15 min).

**2027-05-19 (Wednesday)**
- Morning: Spend a leisurely final morning exploring the LX Factory, a creative hub in a repurposed 19th-century industrial complex in Alcântara. Browse independent shops, design studios, vintage markets (especially lively on weekends, but open on weekdays too), and enjoy a specialty coffee at one of its hip cafés. It's a great spot to pick up unique souvenirs.
- Afternoon: Return to central Lisbon for a final exploration of the Baixa-Chiado area. Visit the Santa Justa Lift (Elevador de Santa Justa) for rooftop views, then wander Rua Augusta and the Praça do Comércio by the waterfront. If time allows, pop into the Lisbon Story Centre for a multimedia overview of the city's history — a nice wrap-up to the historical theme of the trip.
- Evening: Farewell dinner at a special mid-range restaurant — consider Cervejaria Ramiro for a classic Lisbon seafood feast, or book a table at a wine bar in Príncipe Real for a final taste of Portuguese wines paired with petiscos. End the night with a walk along the Tagus waterfront.
- Notes: LX Factory's Sunday market is the most vibrant, but weekday visits are quieter and more relaxed. If your departure flight is on May 20 or later, consider a half-day trip to Sintra or Cascais by train from Rossio station as an optional Day 5 alternative. Keep the evening flexible if you have an early departure the following day.
